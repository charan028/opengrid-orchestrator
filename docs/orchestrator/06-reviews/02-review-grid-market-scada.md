## 1. Bottom line

1. **Not ready for a real ERCOT resource.** For an ADER registered as an ALR, ERCOT's set point is a binding instruction
   on the aggregate net load of every member premise; the engine treats it as a lower-tier "call" that firm contracts may
   squeeze, prices deliberate deviation, and shows ERCOT capability that is already sold to other buyers.
2. **The core firm service can overload banks while reporting compliance.** The deferral law regulates kW against kVA
   ratings, ignores phases and the fleet's own reactive power, double-counts fleet charging in the recharge limit, and its
   PI unwinds whenever another service relieves the bank — which the no-stacking M&V then books as a deferral failure.
3. **Several defaults would put Base in the headlines of the next grid emergency:** a reserve raise (with charging) at
   EEA2, integral trims that cancel the hubs' autonomous frequency response, zone and fleet stops that wait for a second
   approver, unverified ride-through settings, and 15 minutes of fallback schedules that no utility can stop.
4. **`MOBILE_TEEEF` as specified** (grid-parallel mode, "planned support", Base-initiated energization) conflicts with
   PURA §39.918's isolation and utility-operation rules, and its cold-load plan overloads a 1 MW unit.
5. **The SCADA document is the strongest piece of the set.** With the ten changes of §5 — none of which drops a service —
   the set becomes reviewable by a utility and a QSE for a pilot.

## 2. Findings

**Counts: Critical 5 · High 16 · Medium 29 · Low 10 · total 60.** Ordered by severity, then by risk within a
severity. *Critical:* in normal operation the design would violate an ERCOT rule or statute, overload equipment or create
a safety hazard. *High:* a violation, overload or loss of firm delivery is likely in foreseeable conditions. *Medium:*
wrong economics, weak evidence or operability gaps that will surface in a pilot. *Low:* inconsistencies that tests or
implementers will trip on.

## 4. What would satisfy me, per service type

**`HOME`.** A bench test on real hub firmware showing the reserve floor and islanding behaviour enforced locally with the
cloud disconnected (register Q2). Storm holds and reserve raises planned before risk windows and never implemented by
grid charging during an EEA; an explicit reserve-recovery rule inside need windows (GRD-047). A 90-day pilot report with
zero reserve violations, backup energy served per outage, and every opt-out applied within one cycle.

**`ERCOT_ENERGY`.** A decided registration model per territory (ALR or NCLR; LSE, QSE and Resource Entity named). For
ALR: an ADER-level NPC regulator that tracks the UDSP within CLREDP/SPD tolerances in a 30-day replay that includes
house-load noise, EV starts, partner and deferral actions on member hubs and frequency events (GRD-001, -009, -022);
MPC/LPC and ramp telemetry that never exceed ledger-free, guardian-permitted capability (GRD-002, -013); COP and offers
consistent with telemetry (GRD-023); QSE runbooks for ICCP loss, VDIs and EEA (GRD-014, -021); shadow settlement
reconciled to ERCOT statements within 1% (GRD-040).

**`ERCOT_AS`.** ALR: AS delivered through SCED and the UDSP with no separate deployment call; capability telemetry
equal to what the ledger can hold for the product's duration (ECRS 1 h per NPRR1282, Non-Spin 4 h). NCLR: XML
deployment and recall, deployed until recall, 95–150% performance band, failure counter and suspension alarm (GRD-017).
Per-ADER qualified-MW caps (GRD-057). A replayed EEA day in which no awarded or deployed AS is withdrawn by a reserve
policy (GRD-004).

**`PARTNER_CAPACITY`.** The contract model per partner — toll with utility-controlled charge and discharge, or event
program — with the tolled kW reserved continuously (GRD-015); a cycle budget and degradation accounting (GRD-043); a 4CP
coincidence plan that meets each partner's response time inside the fleet ramp table (GRD-012); meter-level P10
declarations inside transformer caps (GRD-032); where the partner is also the ADER's QSE, the partner's QSE decides
between its services (GRD-024); M&V methods aligned so no kWh is counted by two counterparties (GRD-037).

**`DIST_DEFERRAL`.** Regulation on kVA or per-phase current against the ratings actually used by the utility, with the
fleet's reactive power included (GRD-003, -006); the recharge law with add-back (GRD-007); a performance definition
consistent with the controller (GRD-008); plausibility rules tuned to each RTU's deadbands and an OMS switching feed
(GRD-026, -027); the list of other loops on the bank (GRD-028); SB 415 terms for TDU contracts (GRD-048); a field pilot
on one bank with ≥ 15 events (the reviewers' protocol) and SCADA step checks passing per phase.

**`LARGE_LOAD`.** Signed trigger path and contracted scope as specified; ADER telemetry and COP showing the capacity
withdrawn from ERCOT before a stress event, not during it (GRD-002); delivered-kWh coincidence reports; the SB 6 credit
question left to the business case, as decided.

**`PIPELINE_AC`.** A transmission-owner data agreement for real line current at 2–4 s and the shift factor of the
corridor partition's injection on that line (GRD-034); open-loop kW schedules when the shift factor is unknown;
achieved ΔI reported next to delivered kW; recharge placement honoured; H3 alerts with before/after evidence; RMU and
coupon readings passed through unchanged.

**`MOBILE_TEEEF`.** A statute-shaped profile — island-forming only, TDU-operated, qualifying outage declared by the
lessee, no ERCOT market or telemetry (GRD-005); no Base-originated energization (GRD-019); island plans at the measured
cold-load factor with vendor short-time ratings (GRD-018); per-site grounding and temporary-overvoltage study,
protection settings and the synchronization or dead-bus procedure agreed with the utility and signed off by the
licensed field engineer (register Q20); lease billing reviewed against §39.918(c).

**`PJM_CAPACITY`.** ComEd's residential PLC method confirmed (interval data vs class average); dispatch limited to meter
net load unless export is paid (GRD-049); no double counting with PJM demand response through a CSP; a back-test on a
recorded PJM summer with the value realized one delivery year later.

## 5. Top 10 changes, ranked by risk reduction

1. **Treat ERCOT instructions as hard constraints and keep commitments out of ERCOT's reach before the fact.** An
   ADER-level NPC regulator tracking the UDSP for on-line ALRs; ISO instructions at L2-equivalent precedence; MPC/LPC,
   ramps, AS capability, offers and COP computed from ledger-free, guardian-permitted capacity; separate ALR and NCLR
   profiles (GRD-001, -002, -013, -017, -022, -023). Removes the largest compliance exposure and makes "one kWh, one
   buyer" true at the ISO boundary.
2. **Regulate what the transformer rating protects.** kVA or per-phase current with the fleet's reactive power
   included, unit-typed ratings, phase in the topology (GRD-003, -006). Removes systematic under-relief of the core firm
   service.
3. **Replace the EEA reserve raise with pre-positioning and an EEA posture** that never adds grid charging or withdraws
   awarded AS during an emergency (GRD-004). Removes the most visible reputational and compliance risk.
4. **Make `MOBILE_TEEEF` statute-compliant and life-safe.** Island-forming under TDU operation in qualifying outages, no
   Base-originated energization, cold-load planning at measured factors (GRD-005, -018, -019).
5. **Protect autonomous grid support and verify protection settings.** Freeze integrators, substitution and trust
   penalties during frequency and voltage events; declare PFR; frequency-gate injection stops; read back IEEE 1547
   settings fleet-wide and gate rollouts on them (GRD-009, -020, -025).
6. **Fix the two control-law defects in the deferral service.** Add-back in the recharge law; a PI and M&V definition
   that do not fight when other services relieve the bank (GRD-007, -008).
7. **Make stops single-person at every scope, keep releases two-person, and pre-authorize downward re-declarations**
   (GRD-010, -041).
8. **Publish one ramp governance table.** Firm response vs fleet caps vs SCED ramp telemetry, a 4CP coincidence plan,
   fleet-level recharge timing outside ERCOT's net peak (GRD-012, -013, -045).
9. **Model market roles and contract semantics per territory and partner.** NOIE as LSE/QSE, tolling, dual
   participation through the partner's QSE, per-utility tariffs, SB 415 reservation calendars (GRD-015, -016, -024,
   -048).
10. **Harden loss-of-communication behaviour.** One normative autonomy and QSE link-loss policy, no NPC step without an
    ERCOT instruction, randomized fallback boundaries, an independent utility stop path, and a staffed QSE desk with VDI
    handling (GRD-011, -014, -021).
