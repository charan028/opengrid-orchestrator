Integrations

Everything OpenGrid exchanges with systems outside its own processes: what comes in, what goes out, how often, where it lands and what uses it. Every MQTT topic below lives under the configured root [mqtt].topic_root (production og/v1), written <root>; the full topic table with QoS and schemas is in MQTT topics.

ERCOT public API feeds

og-feeds polls the ERCOT public reports API ([feeds.ercot].base_url) with a subscription key and an ERCOT API account. The key and account are read from environment variables named in the config (subscription_key_env, subscription_key_secondary_env, username_env, password_env); their values live in the environment files under /etc/opengrid. Requests share one token bucket (budget_requests_per_min = 24) and one circuit breaker per source.

At r3.4 the scheduler polls exactly the products listed in [feeds.ercot].products. A product not listed is never polled, whatever its own switch says; an unknown product id in the list stops og-feeds at start-up with a configuration error. When the key is absent from the file, every known product is polled. A failed or skipped poll is retried with a capped exponential backoff from 5 minutes up to 60 minutes, never later than the product's normal cadence.

EIA and NWS

EIA v2 ([feeds.eia])
Hourly demand (type=D) for respondent ERCO from /electricity/rto/region-data/data/. API key read from the environment variable named by api_key_env (EIA_API_KEY). It is polled only from inside the ERCOT np6-345-cd poll when that product's breaker blocks the request, so it is idle while ERCOT is healthy. An old EIA row does not raise ALR-FEED-STALE unless the ERCOT product it backs is itself unavailable. Staleness eia_fresh_s 10800. Stored in og.feed_obs.
NWS ([feeds.nws])
api.weather.gov, no key; a descriptive User-Agent is sent. The grid point (grid_point) is resolved once at start-up, then the hourly forecast is polled every hour with If-Modified-Since. Staleness nws_fresh_s 10800. Stored in og.feed_obs; used by the forecast. Not firm-blocking.

ERCOT AS deployment instructions (r3.4.1, D-35)

og-feeds polls ERCOT's ancillary-service dispatch instructions (DEPLOY_AS and RECALL_AS for ECRS, RRS, Reg-Up/Down and Non-Spin) every 5 s, first in each tick and independent of the public-feed polls. The source is the ercot_mms adapter (EWS get VDIs, acknowledged with change VDIs), built from [feeds.ercot_as_poll.mms]. Until go-live the endpoint is the ogsim MMS simulator on the market sim (http://127.0.0.1:8090/mms/ews/); a real MMS endpoint replaces it by configuration only. Nothing is ever submitted to ERCOT.

Which award
An instruction names an ERCOT resource and an AS type. [feeds.ercot_as_poll.awards] maps "<resource>:<AS type>" to the ERCOT_AS contract, and the obligation of that contract whose window covers the instruction's start is deployed. No match is 404; two matches are 409.
Same rules as the operator
Each instruction goes through the same core as POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments (origin ERCOT_POLL): 404 no award, 409 not deployable, already deployed, over the product's duration or over the awarded kW, 422 malformed. It deploys only ERCOT_AS awards, never the utility TOLLING contract. A recall ends the deployment it names.
Idempotent and ordered
The instruction id is the idempotency key: a duplicate delivery never deploys twice and gets the same answer. Each batch is processed in issue order. A recall that arrives before its deployment is held, and the deployment is then never started. An instruction first seen more than 5 minutes after issue is refused as late.
Trace and answer
Every decision is traced on stream ercot_as_poll (origin ERCOT_POLL) before ERCOT is answered ACCEPT, or REJECT with <status> <reason code>.
Alerts and retries
ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED for each refused instruction. ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-FAILED after 3 failed polls and ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-STALE after 60 s without a good poll; both clear on the next good one. A failed poll is retried after 5, 10, 20, 40 and then every 60 s.
Switch
[feeds.ercot_as_poll].enabled ships false; the release manager enables it in production. On its own it changes nothing: with no instruction nothing is written, and committed AS awards keep their 0 kW hold until ERCOT (or an operator) deploys them.
Simulating ERCOT
On the /ogsim/ control page, scenarios ercot_as_01_ecrs_deploy to ercot_as_07_exceed_award (normal ECRS, RRS event, recall with out-of-order delivery, duplicate, malformed, unknown award, over the award) or single ercot_as_* injections. The simulator adds 1-4 s delivery jitter, occasional duplicates and out-of-order batches, and lists every instruction with our answer at /mms/admin/vdis.

Battery telemetry, acks and DEVICE-INFO

Telemetry <root>/tel/<zone>/<bank_id>/<hub_id>
QoS 0, every 10 s per hub ([fleet].telemetry_interval_s). og-engine's ingest loop updates the fleet twin and writes og.hub_state and og.telemetry (kept 14 days); og-guardian subscribes separately for its own hub-state read. A hub is stale after 25 s ([health].hub_stale_s) and offline after 60 s (hub_offline_s); r3.4 uses these two values everywhere (health, fleet twin, screens). A missed sample is covered by the next one.
Acks <root>/ack/<hub_id>
QoS 1, one per received command batch; stored in og.command_ack; read by og-engine and og-guardian.
DEVICE-INFO <root>/hub/<hub_id>/info
QoS 1, retained; topic set by [mqtt].device_info_topic (default hub/+/info); schema device_info.schema.json. Handled by og-engine through opengrid.fleet.device_info. At r3.4 (H4) the hub is taken from the topic: a payload whose hub_id differs, or an unknown hub, is rejected and traced, nothing written. Reported units, rated kW and kWh, reserve floor and latitude/longitude go only to the og.hub.device_* columns (migration 0043). The seed columns the fleet plans and signs with (units, p_kw, e_kwh, r_kwh, lat, lon) are never changed by a report. A difference raises ALR-DEVICE-RATING-MISMATCH. Every report is traced as DEVICE_INFO or DEVICE_INFO_REJECTED on stream fleet_device_info.

Firmware status (ack/fw)

<root>/ack/fw/<hub_id>, QoS 1, schema firmware_status.schema.json. The hub reports each phase of an update (for example DOWNLOADING, INSTALLING, REBOOTING) with a timestamp. og-engine validates it with its own parser (never the command-ack parser) and hands it to opengrid.firmware.ingest, which updates og.firmware_job and appends og.firmware_job_event. The executor then retries transient failures with backoff, raises ALR-FIRMWARE-HUB-FAILED on a terminal one and halts or completes the campaign. After a successful update the hub republishes DEVICE-INFO with the new firmware version. See firmware maintenance and the firmware playbook.

Utility SCADA and protocol adapters

The only SCADA path running at r3.4 is MQTT: <root>/scada/<bank_id> (bank signals, QoS 0) and <root>/scada/instruction/<bank_id> (utility ESTOP / BLOCK / LIMIT instructions, QoS 1), today published by the simulator ogsim.scada. og-engine stores readings in og.feed_obs (source scada) for the DIST_DEFERRAL PI loop and guardian G-03; og-guardian subscribes to instructions itself (G-15, K5). Health watches freshness (ALR-SCADA-SILENT, ALR-SCADA-SILENT-BANK, DIST_DEFERRAL_OPEN_LOOP).

opengrid.integrations holds production protocol backends behind one ScadaSource interface (docs docs/orchestrator/07-delivery/integrations/protocol-adapters.md). They emit the same ScadaBankSignal / ScadaUtilityInstruction models and, in production, republish on the same MQTT topics through MqttBridgeSink, so the guardian keeps its independent subscription.

Utility grid-control link (disabled)

From r3.4.1 a utility's control system (EMS), with Austin Energy first, can control OpenGrid directly (D-34; spec docs/orchestrator/07-delivery/integrations/grid-link.md). It sends D-29 toll calls (discharge only, at most 90 minutes) and the existing L2 LIMIT and BLOCK, per bank or zone. It reads back available and delivered kW, the call state, SoC (for the utility and per bank) and alarms. og-engine is the DNP3 outstation (IEEE 1815, on the in-house codec, no extra package) and the EMS is the master, over mutual TLS. Real Texas EMS links are usually ICCP: the link's service is protocol-neutral, so an ICCP transport can be added later with a licensed TASE.2 stack.

Toll calls
The EMS stages setpoint kW, duration and its own call id on analog outputs 0–2, then operates CALL_EXECUTE (CROB 0, select-before-operate). The call goes through the same core call function as the Dispatch screen's utility call and the customer API, with origin GRID_LINK and the EMS call id as the idempotency key. The core checks the tolling obligation and the 90-minute cap, and it writes the deployment, the call ledger, the trace and the operator alert. The outcome shows on the CALL_STATE and CALL_REASON points.
L2 LIMIT / BLOCK
The EMS sets a limit value (AO 16+t) and switches LIMIT or BLOCK per target (CROB 16+2t / 17+2t). A zone target applies to every bank in the zone, and the most restrictive level wins. The instructions are republished on the existing <root>/scada/instruction/<bank_id> topic by MQTT user og_gridlink, so og-engine and the guardian's own L2 port (G-15) see them exactly as SCADA instructions.
Security
Each utility has:
  • a peer IP allow-list;
  • a client-certificate CN allow-list, required whenever it listens on a non-loopback address;
  • its point list as the allow-list for controls;
  • a limit on concurrent associations.
Every inbound command except a heartbeat is traced before it takes effect (GRID_LINK_COMMAND, origin GRID_LINK). Refusals are traced as AUTHZ_DENY.
Heartbeat loss
The EMS pulses CROB 2. If none arrives for heartbeat_timeout_s (30 s), then:
  • new toll calls are refused (control status AUTOMATION_INHIBIT);
  • cancels are still accepted;
  • a running call continues to its own end;
  • L2 levels stay as last received.
Configuration
[grid_link] in orchestrator.toml. The master switch and each utility's switch are false. Entries for AUSTIN_ENERGY (port 20001), LCRA (20002) and RAYBURN (20003) ship disabled with placeholder peers. LCRA and RAYBURN have no contract yet.
Simulators
ogsim.scada can send its L2 instructions over the link (scada.yaml grid_link:). The Austin Energy simulator can issue its toll calls over it (utility_aen.yaml channel: grid_link).

Customer site ingest

<root>/site/<customer_id>/<site_id>/meter (customer_site_meter.schema.json) and <root>/corridor/<customer_id>/<corridor_id>/current (pipeline_corridor_current.schema.json), QoS 0, published by the customer's equipment or the og_sim_customer simulator. opengrid.site_ingest validates each payload and checks that its customer and source ids match the topic (one customer cannot write into another's loop), keeps the newest reading per source in memory and flushes in batches on the engine's timer into og.customer_site_meter_reading and og.corridor_current_reading. The DATA_CENTER and PIPELINE_AC closed-loop controllers read them through the profile's feedback_signal_ref. Switched by [site_ingest].enabled, which ships false (on at go-live).

Outbound signed MQTT commands

Nothing reaches a hub unsigned. og-guardian signs command batches (<root>/cmd/<bank_id>/batch), leases (<root>/lease/<hub_id>, retained), calibration commands (cmd/cal) and firmware commands (cmd/fw); og-safestop signs stop ENGAGE events (<root>/stop/<scope>/<id>, retained); a stop RELEASE carries the guardian's signature after two-person approval.

Scheme
Ed25519 (RFC 8032) over the RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS) bytes of the object without its signature and key_id fields; signature encoded base64url without padding; key_id names the public key. Specified in interfaces/crypto.md.
Freshness
Every batch carries epoch, seq, issued_at and expires_at (lease-bounded, fleet.lease_ttl_s 30 s). Hubs verify the signature first, then freshness (K6, G-13). A hub whose lease expires holds its last signed setpoint.
Keys
Guardian: [guardian].key_path = /etc/opengrid/guardian_ed25519.key. Safe stop: [safestop].key_path = /etc/opengrid/safestop_ed25519.key. Trace anchors: /etc/opengrid/trace_anchor_ed25519.key. Only the owning process reads its key; og-api never holds one.
Order
The decision pre-image is traced before signing (G-14, K10); the guardian publishes only after a PASS verdict.

Firmware commands (cmd/fw)

<root>/cmd/fw/<hub_id>, QoS 1, not retained, schema firmware_command.schema.json. A campaign job asks the guardian's firmware hand-off (opengrid.firmware.guardian_flow), which reads every input itself, runs G-36 and signs only on PASS; the signed command is recorded in og.firmware_command and published. Refusals are holds: the engine re-requests after its defer interval, except catalogue refusals, which no waiting fixes. Campaigns are created and approved through the API (/og/api/firmware/campaigns).

ERCOT MMS adapter (disabled)

opengrid.integrations.ercot_mms is a QSE submission client for ERCOT MMS External Web Services: energy and three-part offers and AS offers (BidSet create/cancel), DAM/RTM awards (AwardSet get) and verbal dispatch instructions (VDIs get and acknowledge). A submission is POSTed exactly once; timeouts and 5xx are recorded as errors and never resent.

Submission is off twice over: the market backend defaults to backend = "none", and the factory refuses to build ercot_mms unless [integrations.market].enabled = true. Neither key is set in orchestrator.toml. Nothing is ever submitted to ERCOT. Since r3.4.1 og-feeds builds the same client read-only for AS deployment instructions when [feeds.ercot_as_poll] is enabled; it only reads instructions and answers them. The Markets bid funnel shows an mms count only if an MMS submissions table exists.

Customer API

opengrid.customer_api adds customer-scoped routes under /og/api/customer/: me, contracts, opportunities (priced admission; 409 with reason_code on rejection), obligations, invoices and disputes, obligations/{id}/cancel (direct only before selection, otherwise an operator-reviewed request) and obligations/{id}/renominate. A customer is an Apache account mapped in [api.roles.customer]; every read is scoped to its customer_id (another customer's object answers 404) and it gets 403 on operator and viewer endpoints. Operators see requests at GET /og/api/customer-disputes and GET /og/api/customer-requests.

The routes are mounted only when [api.customer_api].enabled = true. The code default is false; since r3.4.1 the shipped orchestrator.toml sets true (D-33), which also mounts the utility routes. See customer onboarding and APIs.

Utility customer API (r3.4.1, D-33)

A utility issues and follows its own toll calls under /og/api/customer/v1/utility/: GET me, GET obligations (its tolling obligations from today, America/Chicago, and today's window), POST calls, GET calls, GET calls/{id}, POST calls/{id}/cancel and, from r3.4.3, GET delivery-records (the measured delivery of its own calls). The Austin Energy EMS simulator (ogsim.utility_aen) calls it through Apache. Full reference: docs/api/utility-api.md.

Who
An Apache account listed in [api.roles.utility] gets the role utility and acts for the utility_id it maps to (og-util-aen = AUSTIN_ENERGY). Only utilities in [api.utility_api].enabled_utilities may use it: AUSTIN_ENERGY today. og-util-lcra and og-util-rayburn are mapped but not enabled (D-37). Any other identity, and a utility that is not enabled, gets 403. Apache admits the account only under /og/api/customer/, and og-api refuses it (403) on the operator and viewer endpoints.
Scope
Every read and call is limited to the caller's utility_id. Another utility's obligation or call answers 404 exactly like an unknown id, never 403. The attempt is traced as AUTHZ_DENY. The utility can read every call on its own toll, an operator's included, but from r3.4.3 it can cancel or shorten only the calls it issued: any other answers 403 R-CALL-NOT-ISSUER. From r3.4.3 every refused utility request, reads included, is traced as AUTHZ_DENY.
Calls
The body has a signed kw (negative: discharge only), duration_minutes, a required idempotency_key, and optional start_at and obligation_id (default: the toll whose window covers the start). The call goes through the same core as the Dispatch screen's utility call and the grid link, with origin UTILITY. The same checks apply: the 90-minute cap, the window, the committed kW, no overlap and a deployable state. The answer is 201 (200 for a replayed key), or the refusal's status with its reason_code. From r3.4.3 every timestamp needs a UTC offset (422 otherwise), and cancelling an ended call answers 409 R-CALL-ALREADY-ENDED (r3.4.2: R-CALL-CANNOT-EXTEND).
State
ACCEPTED before the start. While the call runs it is ACTIVE until its delivery is measured, then RAMPING or DELIVERING: measured discharge below, or at or above, [dispatch.calls].ramping_fraction (0.9) of the call's kW. COMPLETED after its end or a cancel, and REFUSED. From r3.4.3 (D-38), og-settle's delivery job measures each call in 30 s buckets, so the first measurement comes about a minute in. The status then carries delivered_kw/delivered_kwh, delivery_measured (true only once a measured value or a final verdict exists), delivery_state (UNMEASURED until then, then IN_PROGRESS, and PASS, PARTIAL or FAIL), delivery_reasons, meter_status and delivery_as_of. granted_kw/granted_kwh/granted_description are planned/granted values, never metered: "planned; removed in r3.5; use delivered_*". In r3.4.2 a running call is always ACTIVE, with delivery_measured: false and delivery_state: "UNMEASURED", and only the granted fields exist.
Limits
[dispatch.calls] allows at most 30 calls per rolling hour and 200 per rolling day per account. Accepted and refused calls count; replays do not. Over the limit: 429 R-CALL-RATE-LIMIT.
What operators see
ALR-UTILITY-CALL (info) on each accepted call and each cancel, and ALR-UTILITY-CALL-REFUSED (warning) on a refusal. The toll's row in the Dispatch screen's AS table reads deployed, "by UTILITY · og-util-aen"; Stop deploy ends the call. The trace stream is dispatch_call:og-util-aen.

The routes are mounted with the customer routes by [api.customer_api].enabled. When it is off, every utility path answers 404.
