Overview

What OpenGrid does

OpenGrid is the orchestrator that decides, every few seconds, what each battery in the Base Power fleet should do, and proves afterwards that it kept its promises. It sells the fleet's capacity and energy to customers, commits to deliveries, dispatches the batteries to meet those commitments, and settles and bills what was actually delivered. It never lets a commercial decision override safety, a homeowner's backup reserve or a grid operator's instruction.

The two markets

OpenGrid serves two kinds of market at once (decision D-20). In the free market (FREE, the ERCOT competitive area) it sells energy arbitrage and ERCOT ancillary services and pays the TDSP delivery charge (M1) on energy drawn from the grid to charge. In the regulated market (REGULATED) a vertically integrated utility such as Austin Energy or CPS Energy is the customer: it buys capacity (for example the D-29 toll), and energy stays inside that utility's territory (invariant K15). See Market model.

The processes at a glance

ProcessJob
og-feedsPolls ERCOT public reports (prices every 5 min, AS clearing prices daily, load hourly, wind and solar every 30 min), NWS weather and EIA (standby for system load). Recomputes the 24 h price/load forecast every 15 min.
og-engineOne 2 s loop: fleet twin flush, the 15-minute selector gate, the allocator cycle, and the hand-off of one command batch per bank to the guardian. Also ingests hub telemetry and SCADA over MQTT.
og-guardianIndependently re-checks every batch against the physical, commercial and territory rules (G-01 to G-36) on its own reads, signs what passes with its Ed25519 key, vetoes what does not, and signs two-person safe-stop releases.
og-safestopIndependent stop authority with a stop-only key. Engages scoped stops even when the engine and guardian are down; relays guardian-signed releases.
og-settleEvery 60 s: meters, settles and bills delivered intervals, computes P&L. Also hosts the health/alert evaluation, the invariant checker, trace pruning and trace anchoring.
og-apiThe REST API under /og/api and this console under /og, behind Apache.

Known gaps recorded in the invariants document: G-31's peak-power allowance cannot be used (field-name defect, so every above-continuous setpoint is vetoed; safe), reverse flow is not measured from SCADA, and the AS-hold check runs only while a deployment is active.

Roles and identity

How your identity is resolved

  1. Apache terminates HTTP Basic authentication. It removes any X-Remote-User header the client sent and sets it from the authenticated user.
  2. Apache also sets X-OG-Proxy-Auth to the shared secret OG_API_PROXY_SECRET. og-api believes X-Remote-User only when that header matches (constant-time compare). No secret configured, no header, or a wrong one: 401.
  3. The user name is mapped to a role through [api.roles] in orchestrator.toml (opengrid.api.auth.role_for_identity). The literal accounts operator and viewer map by name; customers must be listed explicitly under [api.roles.customer].
  4. An identity with no role gets 403 from the API. The console (opengrid.ui.role) shows an unmapped or missing identity as viewer (read-only), never as operator, and every write it relays is re-checked by the API.
  5. The allow/deny decision itself goes through the policy in config/authz.toml (deny by default). Denials of audited actions are written to the trace as TRACE_AUTHZ_DENY.

/og/api/health is the deployment liveness probe: it accepts loopback connections only and never reads the identity header.
