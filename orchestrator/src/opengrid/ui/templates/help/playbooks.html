Playbooks: what to do when...

One playbook per situation. Each uses the same layout: how the situation shows on the console, who acts, what the system already does on its own, then the steps with the exact screen and button, what has to be confirmed, how to tell it is over, and when to escalate. Button labels are the ones in the r3.4.3 templates. Where a step cannot be done from the console at r3.4.3, the playbook says so and gives the API path or the deploy/RUNBOOK.md step instead.

The design runbooks RB-* in docs/orchestrator/02-architecture/05-failure-modes-and-recovery.md describe the target platform (for example NATS, EMQX, a separate Safe-Stop Authority host). The r3.4.3 deployment is a single base server with Postgres 17, Mosquitto and ten systemd services (six og-* services and four simulators; og-sim-utility joins ogsim.target once the release manager installs it with deploy/scripts/r343_utility_sim_install.sh). The playbooks below cite an RB-* only for the intent and follow the r3.4.3 code and deploy/RUNBOOK.md for the actual commands.

Never print or paste the contents of /etc/opengrid/*.env, /etc/opengrid/htpasswd, the signing keys under /etc/opengrid, the certificates under /etc/opengrid/certs or /root/opengrid-ui-credentials.txt. Scripts read them; people do not.

Normal operations and the daily plan

How it shows
Control room (/og/): header badge live; the story line ends "0 promises broken today"; the three "Promises kept" tiles (Reserve breaches, kWh sold twice, Commitment switches) read 0. The notification bell says "Nothing needs attention." apart from info-severity notices (for example ALR-UTILITY-CALL when a utility calls, firmware campaign progress). Since the r3.4.1 topology backfill every hub, bank and feeder is mapped, so ALR-XFMR-UNMAPPED is no longer expected. Where the owner enabled the regulated zones, their capacity shows on its own line "Regulated market – no contract: N kW" (pb-regulated-unavailable).
Who acts
Operator on shift; viewers watch.
What the system does automatically
og-engine runs the 2 s allocator and the quarter-hour selector gates (a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) over 96 quarter-hours, shown on Dispatch as "MILP optimizer", with a rule-based fallback); og-guardian checks and signs every batch; og-engine offers each active tolling contract's daily window ([contracts.tolling], 16:30-18:00 CT, today if not started plus tomorrow); og-settle meters, settles, runs the health evaluator every 5 s and, at r3.4.3, verifies measured delivery of every discharge call every 15 s (pb-delivery-alert); the invariant and trace-chain checks run; the nightly backup runs at 03:00. The obligation lifecycle step (COMMITTED to DELIVERING, window-end FULFILLED or SHORTFALL) runs in the background at r3.4.3, one pass at a time with a 30 s limit, so it never delays the 2 s cycle. There is no separate daily-plan approval step at r3.4.3: the "daily plan" is the selector's latest plan.

Steps (start of shift)

  1. /og/: read the story line, the "Promises kept" tiles and the KPIs. Open the bell and read the Safety, Degraded modes and Alerts groups.
  2. /og/health: every row in "Processes" has a recent heartbeat time; no ALR-PROCESS-DOWN; "Hub health" mostly Online; the "Cycle latency (p50/p99)" chart is under budget.
  3. /og/markets: "Freshness & source status" shows each feed LIVE, failures 0, breaker closed.
  4. /og/dispatch: the "Opportunity pipeline" cards (no amber at-risk border), "Latest selector plan" (Mode "MILP optimizer (L-DA)" or "(L-ID)", Gate, Horizon, Solver status), the "Ledger timeline" per scope (header View), and "AS awards & deployment" (awards and tolling obligations held, Risk OK).
  5. Acknowledge what you have read: tick rows in the Alerts panel and press Acknowledge selected (N), then Acknowledge N in the dialog; or Ack on one row. Acknowledging records who looked; it never clears an alert.
  6. /og/billing: set From and To, then Run chain verify (or POST /og/api/trace/verify with body {}).
  7. /og/profitability: today's Net margin and "Forgone upside (lock)". Yesterday's delivery compliance per contract: GET /og/api/delivery/summary?days=1.
Confirmations required
None for reading. A bulk acknowledgement asks once.
Verify recovery
Not applicable; this is the baseline the other playbooks return to.
Escalate when
Any "Promises kept" tile is non-zero; ALR-RESERVE-BREACH, ALR-TRACE-VERIFY-FAILED or ALR-TRACE-QUARANTINED is open; the selector plan shows "Rule-based fallback" repeatedly or ALR-SELECTOR-GATE-FAILED keeps adding rows. Note: since the r3.4.3 K13 fix, an ERCOT_AS or tolling hold sitting at 0 kW while undeployed is no longer counted as a K13 dip; a K13 violation now means a deployed hold or another commitment really delivered below its floor.

Price spike or feed outage (stale feed, NO_NEW_COMMITMENTS)

How it shows
A spike: every load-zone line on /og/markets "Wholesale price ($/MWh)" and the Control room "Market ticker" jump; no alert is raised for the price itself. An outage: ALR-FEED-STALE (warning, "Feed ERCOT:np6-905-cd stale for over 2700s") or ALR-FEED-LGV-EXHAUSTED (critical, breaker open); on the price feed, the degraded mode Feed stale (NO_NEW_COMMITMENTS) shows in the bell as "Degraded mode: Feed stale" and as the page banner "Degraded: ...". Markets shows the feed's breaker OPEN and a rising failure count.
Who acts
Operator; admin if og-feeds itself is down or needs a restart.
What the system does automatically
Spike: committed obligations keep their kW (K13); only L0/L1/L2 overrides or proven infeasibility may reduce one, and the guardian re-checks any such claim (G-19). From r3.4.1 (FR-ING-117, migration 0045) an extreme price is kept and flagged EXTREME_UNCORROBORATED instead of being dropped, until a consecutive posting corroborates it; malformed or out-of-hard-bound values are quarantined; while the latest price is uncorroborated the forecast is marked NOT_FOR_FIRM. Outage: og-feeds opens the product's circuit breaker; EIA backs the ERCOT load feed only while that feed is unavailable. og-feeds polls only the products in [feeds.ercot].products, and a failed or breaker-skipped poll is retried after 5 minutes, doubling up to 60 minutes (never later than the product's own cadence), so a missed daily AS-price poll recovers within the hour instead of the next day. Only the feeds in [health] firm_blocking_feeds (default the price feed ERCOT:np6-905-cd) set NO_NEW_COMMITMENTS; a blocking feed with no status row at all counts as stale. While the mode is active og-engine skips intake (trace INTAKE_SKIPPED) and every selector gate selects nothing new; offers stay OFFERED; committed deliveries continue. Contract admission through the API is not blocked.

Steps

  1. Spike: open /og/dispatch and confirm every Committed/Delivering card keeps its kW and reads "Locked: this promise is kept even if a better price appears (K13)". Nothing to do; within a 30 s refresh /og/profitability shows the non-zero "Forgone upside (lock)".
  2. Outage: /og/markets "Freshness & source status": which feed, its mode (LIVE, SIM, HIST), age, failures, breaker.
  3. /og/health: "Feed freshness" and "Alerts" (filter Rule). Check "Processes" for the feeds heartbeat; a missing one also raises ALR-PROCESS-DOWN.
  4. Admin, if og-feeds is down or wedged: journalctl -u og-feeds --since -10min, then systemctl restart og-feeds. og-feeds also hosts the ERCOT AS instruction poller when it is enabled, so a restart pauses it too (pb-as-poll-refusal). Do not point og-feeds at the market simulator without the lead's OK (deploy/README.md, "Switching live APIs vs. the market simulator").
  5. If the upstream (ERCOT) is down, wait: nothing new is committed, and what is committed is delivered. Tell the desk that new offers will sit in OFFERED.
Confirmations required
None; no operator action changes feed handling from the console.
Verify recovery
The breaker reads closed, the alert clears by itself, the "Feed stale" entry leaves the bell, and the next gate selects again ("Latest selector plan" gate time moves; OFFERED cards move to Selected).
Escalate when
The price feed stays stale into a day-ahead gate; the breaker re-opens after every close; authentication to ERCOT fails (treat as pb-security); a price move reduces any commitment (that would be a K13 breach).

AS award held, then an ERCOT deployment

How it shows
/og/dispatch, panel "AS awards & deployment": one row per awarded ERCOT_AS obligation (and, since D-33, per tolling obligation) with Obligation, Product (ECRS · 1 h hold, Non-Spin NSPIN · 4 h hold; a tolling row reads just TOLLING), Committed, Energy held, State (held or deployed, and when deployed "by <origin> · <who> · <kW>", origin OPERATOR, ERCOT, UTILITY, GRID_LINK, MARKET_SIM or SCENARIO), Risk (OK or AT_RISK), Delivery ("--" while held) and Action (Deploy…, or Utility call… on a tolling row, or Stop deploy when deployed). A held award short of energy raises ALR-ENERGY-SHORTFALL-RISK.
Who acts
og-feeds, when [feeds.ercot_as_poll] is enabled: it applies ERCOT's instruction itself (D-35). Otherwise the operator, on ERCOT's deployment instruction received outside OpenGrid.
What the system does automatically
A held award is granted 0 kW (R-GRANT-AS-HOLD); its capacity stays out of sellable headroom and its homes keep enough energy above reserve for the whole product (ECRS 1 h, Non-Spin 4 h). The guardian signs the 0 kW hold only when its own reads show an ERCOT_AS award with no active deployment (G-19). While a deployment is active the allocator discharges the award up to its committed kW (or the call's requested kW, if smaller); at its end time the award returns to a 0 kW hold. Every deployment, whatever its origin, goes through the one call path opengrid.calls (D-33) with the same checks and is recorded in the call ledger og.dispatch_call. ERCOT deployment instructions are ingested automatically only while the og-feeds poller is enabled ([feeds.ercot_as_poll], off in the repo). It applies each DEPLOY_AS / RECALL_AS with the same rules as an operator's deployment. It raises ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED for each refused instruction, and ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-FAILED or ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-STALE when the poll itself fails (see ERCOT AS deployment instructions and pb-as-poll-refusal). og-settle verifies the measured delivery of each deployment (pb-delivery-alert) and, at r3.4.3, settles ERCOT_AS capacity at the cleared day-ahead MCPC for the delivery hour (price flag MCPC; without an MCPC observation it falls back to the opportunity's price, flag OPPORTUNITY_PRICE, and logs it).

Steps

  1. /og/dispatch, "AS awards & deployment": find the award's row; check State held and Risk OK.
  2. Press Deploy… on that row. It opens no dialog: it selects the award in the form under the table (field Deploy) and focuses Duration.
  3. Set Duration (min, default 15) within the product (the hint reads "Up to 60 min for ECRS."; "Up to 240 min for NSPIN." for Non-Spin) and Reason (prefilled "Operator ERCOT AS deployment"), then Propose deployment. A duration over the cap is refused on the spot: "Duration must be between 1 and 60 minutes for ECRS."
  4. Read the dialog "Confirm ERCOT AS deployment": "Deploy award <id> (<product>) for N minutes (<reason>)" and "This is step 1 of 2. No award changes state until you confirm." Press Confirm deployment (or Cancel). The result line under the panel reads "Deployment <id> is active." A refusal reads "Refused by the API: <reason code>: <detail>".
  5. Reload /og/dispatch (the table is not live): State reads deployed "by OPERATOR · <you> · <kW>", the Action is Stop deploy and the Delivery cell reads "pending", then the measured result. Watch "Real-time grants & substitutions" and the ledger for the award's bank.
  6. To end early: Stop deploy on the row; the dialog "Confirm stop deployment" reads "Stop active ERCOT_AS deployment <id> early"; press Stop deployment. Result: "Deployment <id> stopped." This ends a deployment of any origin, including one ERCOT's poller started.
  7. API equivalent: POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments with {"obligation_id": "<id>", "duration_minutes": 15, "reason": "..."} (optional requested_kw, negative, and idempotency_key); DELETE /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments/{deployment_id}; active deployments with GET /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments; the call ledger, refusals included, with GET /og/api/dispatch/calls.
Confirmations required
One operator, one confirm dialog (no countdown). The call path refuses with a reason code: no such award (404 R-CALL-NOT-FOUND), a fleet-wide deployment (409 R-CALL-FLEET-WIDE, it would need two people), an award not in a deployable state (409 R-CALL-STATE "obligation is ..., not deployable"), a duration over the product (409 R-CALL-DURATION-CAP), a start outside the reservation window (409 R-CALL-OUTSIDE-WINDOW), a second call overlapping an active one (409 R-CALL-OVERLAP), more than the committed kW (409 R-CALL-OVER-COMMITTED), a positive (charging) kW (422 R-CALL-CHARGE-REFUSED) and the per-principal rate limit (429 R-CALL-RATE-LIMIT, [dispatch.calls] 30 per hour, 200 per day).
Verify recovery
After the end time or a stop, State reads held again and the grant returns to 0 kW. /og/billing "Trace explorer" shows DISPATCH_CALL (with kind AS) and DISPATCH_CALL_END on stream dispatch_call:<user>; the operator-action log has AS_DEPLOYMENT:<obligation id>. GET /og/api/delivery/records/{deployment_id} shows the final result (PASS, PARTIAL or FAIL).
Escalate when
The award is AT_RISK when ERCOT deploys it; the deployment is refused for a reason you cannot resolve; delivered kW stays well below committed kW during the deployment (ALR-DELIVERY-SHORTFALL, see pb-delivery-alert and pb-at-risk).

ERCOT AS instruction refused or not confirmed (AS-POLL, D-35)

How it shows
Only while [feeds.ercot_as_poll] enabled = true (off in the repo; the release manager enables it with the simulator URL, D-35). ALR-ERCOT-AS-PROCESSING-FAILED (critical, r3.4.3) when one instruction raised an error while being applied (retried every poll, unacknowledged; check journalctl -u og-feeds and the database, tell the lead if it persists). ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED (warning), one per refused instruction, summary "ERCOT <service> instruction <id> refused (<status> <reason code>)". ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-FAILED (warning, "ERCOT AS instruction poll failing (N in a row)") after 3 consecutive failed polls; ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-STALE (critical, "No ERCOT AS instructions read since ...: deployments may be missed") once no poll has succeeded for 60 s. In both cases the award's row on /og/dispatch stays held although ERCOT expects it deployed.
Who acts
Operator, with the QSE desk; admin for og-feeds, the award map and the ERCOT connection.
What the system does automatically
Every 5 s og-feeds reads the unacknowledged instructions of the last 15 minutes over the ercot_mms adapter and applies each through the same call path as Deploy… (origin ERCOT_POLL, principal ercot:<backend>, source shown as ERCOT). It traces every answer before acknowledging it to ERCOT, on stream ercot_as_poll (AS_INSTRUCTION_ACCEPTED, AS_INSTRUCTION_REFUSED, AS_INSTRUCTION_DUPLICATE, AS_INSTRUCTION_SUPERSEDED, AS_INSTRUCTION_NOTED, AS_RECALL_APPLIED). The instruction id is the idempotency key, so a re-delivery never deploys twice. A recall that arrives before its deployment is held up to 300 s and the deployment is then never started. Its own refusals are R-ERCOT-AS-MALFORMED (422), R-ERCOT-AS-NO-AWARD (404: no [feeds.ercot_as_poll.awards] entry for "<resource>:<service>", or no award of that contract covers the start), R-ERCOT-AS-AMBIGUOUS-AWARD (409), R-ERCOT-AS-LATE (409, first seen more than 300 s after issue) and R-ERCOT-AS-NOTHING-TO-RECALL (422); everything else is the call path's own R-CALL-* code. It never touches a TOLLING contract. A failed poll is retried after 5, 10, 20, 40 s, then every 60 s; the next good poll clears the FAILED and STALE alerts. ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED is not cleared automatically.

Steps

  1. Open the alert on /og/health (filter Rule ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED); note the instruction id, resource, service, MW and reason code in the detail.
  2. Read the answer ERCOT received: /og/billing "Trace explorer", stream ercot_as_poll; and the ledger row with GET /og/api/dispatch/calls.
  3. Tell the QSE desk at once that the instruction was refused and why: ERCOT expects the capacity and received a negative acknowledgement.
  4. If ERCOT's deployment is valid and the refusal was OpenGrid's (R-ERCOT-AS-NO-AWARD for a missing map entry, R-ERCOT-AS-LATE for a delayed poll): deploy the award by hand on /og/dispatch with Deploy… for the remaining duration (pb-as-deploy). A refusal the call path gave (R-CALL-OVERLAP, R-CALL-STATE, R-CALL-DURATION-CAP, R-CALL-OVER-COMMITTED) would be refused again: resolve it with the desk instead.
  5. POLL-FAILED or POLL-STALE, admin: journalctl -u og-feeds --since -10min | grep -i 'ERCOT AS'; check og-feeds is running and the [feeds.ercot_as_poll.mms] endpoint answers. Meanwhile the operator deploys from ERCOT's instructions received through the desk, as when the poller is off.
  6. Admin, for a missing award map entry: add "<resource>:<SERVICE>" = "<contract id>" under [feeds.ercot_as_poll.awards] through a release.
Confirmations required
A manual deployment needs its own confirm dialog; configuration changes need the lead's OK.
Verify recovery
FAILED and STALE clear on the next good poll; new instructions trace AS_INSTRUCTION_ACCEPTED and the award row turns deployed "by ERCOT". Acknowledge the REFUSED alert once the desk has the reason.
Escalate when
STALE stays open during an awarded hour; refusals repeat for the same resource; ERCOT disputes an acknowledgement; any sign that a TOLLING obligation was deployed by ERCOT (it must never be).

Utility toll call (D-29)

How it shows
A tolling obligation (REGULATED_CAPACITY, contract variant TOLLING) appears as a card in the /og/dispatch pipeline; the "Ledger timeline" draws it in the "Held (AS/toll)" series and the text reads "N kW held for AS/tolling awards (reserved, 0 kW granted until deployed)". Once committed it also has a row in "AS awards & deployment" (there is no separate tolling table): Product TOLLING (no hold-hours suffix), Committed in kW, Energy held "--", State held, Risk normally OK, Delivery "--" and a Utility call… button in the Action column; the Deploy list in the form under the table reads "<id> · TOLLING (utility call)". The panel's lead line reads "A tolling obligation (D-29) is deployed by the utility's call, up to 90 minutes, never overlapping an active one."
Who acts
The utility calls. At r3.4.3 Austin Energy calls through the utility API by itself (pb-utility-call); an operator issues the call on the utility's behalf when the utility phones it in.
What the system does automatically
og-engine offers one OFFERED opportunity per day for each ACTIVE tolling contract, for the [contracts.tolling] window (16:30-18:00 America/Chicago, days_ahead = 1, days = "ALL"), through normal admission. It is idempotent, never re-offers a window that overlaps a COMMITTED/DELIVERING obligation of the same contract (K13), and reserves nothing when the window length differs from the product rule's duration (90 min) or the rule has no size (logged by og-engine). The selector commits it as a regulated capacity hold; the allocator holds it at 0 kW until called, and while called caps it at the call's kW; settlement pays availability. The Austin substation battery feeder has a 20 MW/min ramp ceiling and each Austin home-bank feeder (feeder-LZ_AEN-00, -01) 7 MW/min (G-06, [guardian.feeder_ramp_ceiling_kw_per_min_by_feeder]); since r3.4.1 the guardian anchors the toll ramp (G-04, G-05, G-06, G-32) on the last signed setpoint while its lease is live, so a 20 MW ramp between telemetry reports is not vetoed; since r3.4.3 the engine steps from the same signed anchor, the G-04 bound is one cycle, and a veto or safe stop re-anchors both sides on telemetry.

Steps (operator on the utility's behalf)

  1. /og/dispatch, "AS awards & deployment": press Utility call… on the tolling row. No dialog opens: the form under the table selects it in Deploy ("<id> · TOLLING (utility call)"), puts the cursor in Duration, and the hint reads "Up to 90 min for TOLLING."
  2. Set Duration (default 15; at most 90, otherwise "Duration must be between 1 and 90 minutes for TOLLING.") and replace the prefilled Reason "Operator ERCOT AS deployment" with the utility's reference; press Propose deployment. The dialog is titled "Confirm ERCOT AS deployment" (the same dialog as an AS award) and reads "Issue the utility's call on tolling obligation <id> for N minutes (<reason>)" and "This is step 1 of 2. No award changes state until you confirm." Press Confirm deployment.
  3. The result line reads "Deployment <id> is active." (or "Refused by the API: <reason code>: <detail>", for example an overlap). Reload the page (the table is not live): State reads deployed "by OPERATOR · <you> · <kW>", the Action is Stop deploy, and the Delivery cell reads "pending", then the measured result (click it for the Measured delivery drawer). Watch "Real-time grants & substitutions" and the ledger for the carrying banks.
  4. End early if the utility releases the call: Stop deploy; the dialog "Confirm stop deployment" reads "Stop active ERCOT_AS deployment <id> early" (the same wording for a toll); press Stop deployment. Result: "Deployment <id> stopped."
  5. API equivalent: POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments with {"obligation_id": "<id>", "duration_minutes": 90, "reason": "<utility reference>"}; the deployment reason is stored prefixed "utility call: " and the answer carries kind: "UTILITY_CALL". End with DELETE /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments/{deployment_id}.
Confirmations required
One confirm dialog (no countdown). The same R-CALL-* refusals as an AS deployment apply; the cap is the obligation's product rule (TOLLING 90 min), and a utility call must also end inside the reservation window.
Verify recovery
The row returns to held at the call's end; the grant returns to 0 kW; the trace shows DISPATCH_CALL with kind UTILITY_CALL and DISPATCH_CALL_END on stream dispatch_call:<user>, and the operator-action log has UTILITY_CALL:<obligation id>. The delivery record (GET /og/api/delivery/records/{deployment_id}) reads PASS.
Escalate when
No tolling obligation exists for today's window (check og-engine's log for "tolling window does not match the product rule duration" or "no sized product rule"); the refusal is R-CALL-STATE "obligation is OFFERED, not deployable" (the selector has not committed it); delivery falls short during the window (pb-delivery-alert).

A utility call arrives through the utility API (D-33)

How it shows
The bell and /og/health show ALR-UTILITY-CALL (info), summary "UTILITY call from og-util-aen: N kW for M min from <start>" (a grid-link call reads "GRID_LINK call from grid_link:<utility>"). On /og/dispatch (reload: the AS table is not live) the tolling row in "AS awards & deployment" turns deployed "by UTILITY · og-util-aen · -N kW", its Action becomes Stop deploy, its Delivery cell fills with the measured result, and the carrying banks ramp. A refused call raises ALR-UTILITY-CALL-REFUSED (warning) with its reason code, for example R-CALL-OUTSIDE-WINDOW. A cancel or shortening from the utility raises another ALR-UTILITY-CALL ("... cancelled call ..." or "... shortened call ...").
Who acts
The utility (its system calls POST /og/api/customer/v1/utility/calls); the operator watches, answers the utility's questions and may end the call.
What the system does automatically
The utility API runs the same core call function as the operator's button (opengrid.calls, origin UTILITY, principal og-util-aen). The call is discharge only (kw negative), at most 90 min, inside that utility's own reservation window, never overlapping another call, within the committed kW, idempotent per idempotency_key (a resend answers 200 with the original call) and rate limited per principal. With no obligation_id the obligation whose window covers the start is called. It is traced (DISPATCH_CALL on stream dispatch_call:og-util-aen) before it takes effect, recorded in og.dispatch_call (refusals too) and audited in og.operator_action. The toll ramps under the guardian's feeder ceilings (pb-toll-call); since r3.4.3 the engine steps the substation set from its last guardian-signed setpoint, one cycle's G-04 step at a time, and re-anchors on telemetry after a veto, a lapsed lease or a safe stop. The call's status for the utility (GET .../calls/{call_id}) comes from measured delivery at r3.4.3: ACCEPTED, RAMPING (below 90% of target, [dispatch.calls] ramping_fraction), DELIVERING, ACTIVE (running but not measured yet), COMPLETED or REFUSED, with the measured kW and kWh (pb-delivery-alert). The full interface is under Utility API and how utility calls work.

Steps

  1. Read the alert; open /og/dispatch and find the tolling row "by UTILITY".
  2. Follow the call: the tolling row's Delivery column (click it for the Measured delivery drawer), GET /og/api/dispatch/calls?utility_id=AUSTIN_ENERGY (ledger, newest first) and GET /og/api/delivery/records?utility_id=AUSTIN_ENERGY (measured delivery). The utility reads the same record at /og/api/customer/v1/utility/delivery-records/{call_id}.
  3. Watch the ramp: the 20 MW set steps each 2 s cycle from its last guardian-signed setpoint (r3.4.3), so Commanded rises steadily with few or no vetoes. A long run of G-04 vetoes on the set is not expected any more; if it happens, capture the verdicts and escalate (how utility calls work).
  4. If the utility asks to stop, it cancels with POST .../calls/{call_id}/cancel. If it cannot, end it for them: Stop deploy on the tolling row, then Stop deployment in the "Confirm stop deployment" dialog ("Stop active ERCOT_AS deployment <id> early"; the wording is the same for a toll). The result reads "Deployment <id> stopped." and the utility sees the call COMPLETED.
  5. Refused call: read the reason code in the alert; tell the utility what it means (for example R-CALL-OVERLAP: a call already covers part of the window; R-CALL-DURATION-CAP: over 90 min; R-CALL-CHARGE-REFUSED: kw not negative; R-CALL-NOT-FOUND: no deployable tolling obligation covers the start).
  6. To rehearse on the simulated Austin Energy EMS (og-sim-utility, ogsim.utility_aen): start a scenario from /ogsim/, for example utility-aen-normal-call, utility-aen-cancel-mid-call, utility-aen-overlap-call, utility-aen-over-cap-call or utility-aen-charge-refused.
Confirmations required
None for the utility's own calls (the API is its authorisation); ending a call from the console uses the "Confirm stop deployment" dialog.
Verify recovery
At the call's end the row returns to held, grants to 0 kW, and the final delivery record reads PASS with meter status CORROBORATED on the metered substation bank.
Escalate when
A delivery alert opens during the call (pb-delivery-alert); refusals repeat for a call the utility believes valid; an unknown account appears in AUTHZ_DENY traces for the utility API (treat as pb-security); LCRA or RAYBURN try to call (they have no contract, pb-regulated-unavailable).

Grid-link heartbeat lost (DNP3, D-34)

How it shows
Only where the grid link is enabled: it is off in the shipped config ([grid_link] enabled = false and each utility's own enabled = false); og-engine starts it at startup once both are on, and r3.4.3 ships only a loopback test enablement. og-engine logs an error "grid link state change" with state HEARTBEAT_LOST, and the trace has GRID_LINK_STATE HEARTBEAT_LOST on stream grid_link:<utility>. The utility's EMS sees the binary input ALARM_HEARTBEAT_LOST set and its heartbeat count frozen. No console alert rule is raised for it at r3.4.3. New EMS calls are answered INHIBITED (traced as GRID_LINK_DENY, decision AUTHZ_DENY).
Who acts
Operator, with the utility's control room; admin for og-engine, certificates and the firewall.
What the system does automatically
The link is healthy only while the EMS heartbeat arrives within heartbeat_timeout_s (30 s). When it is lost: new toll calls are refused; cancels are still accepted; a call already running continues to its own end (bounded by the 90 min product); L2 LIMIT and BLOCK levels stay exactly as last received and are not lifted. The link returns to healthy on the next heartbeat (trace GRID_LINK_STATE HEALTHY). Across an og-engine restart the L2 levels are rebuilt from the link's own trace (L2_RESTORED).

Steps

  1. Confirm: journalctl -u og-engine --since -10min | grep -i 'grid link' (listening, associations, refusals, state changes).
  2. Call the utility's control room: is their EMS master down, or is the TLS association failing (look for GRID_LINK_DENY traces with a connection reason)?
  3. If a running call must stop, end it on /og/dispatch with Stop deploy (the row shows "by GRID_LINK").
  4. Review active L2 levels on /og/fleet (utility instructions issued by UTILITY_GRID_LINK); agree with the utility whether any must be lifted. Only the utility lifts its own levels.
  5. Admin: check og-engine is running, the certificates under /etc/opengrid/certs are valid, and the firewall still admits the EMS addresses. The loopback test (deploy/scripts/grid_link_enable_loopback.sh --test-call, --disable) is for the release manager only.
Confirmations required
Stopping a call uses the normal confirm dialog. Enabling, disabling or reconfiguring the link is a release-manager change with the lead and the owner.
Verify recovery
Trace GRID_LINK_STATE HEALTHY; the EMS sees ALARM_HEARTBEAT_LOST clear and the heartbeat count rising; its next call is accepted (ALR-UTILITY-CALL).
Escalate when
The link stays down into the tolling window; the utility cannot lift an L2 BLOCK it set; GRID_LINK_DENY traces show an unknown peer or certificate (treat as pb-security). Background: Grid link.

Commitment AT_RISK or SHORTFALL: substitution and restore

How it shows
On /og/dispatch the card gets an amber border, with a falling "energy margin" and "depletes in"; ALR-ENERGY-SHORTFALL-RISK (critical) opens when energy is the cause. At r3.4.3 a called obligation is also flagged AT_RISK when its measured delivery is short (ALR-DELIVERY-SHORTFALL or ALR-DELIVERY-NONE, reason R-DELIVERY-MEASURED-SHORTFALL; pb-delivery-alert). A SHORTFALL card moves to "Fulfilled / shortfall" and reads "Delivered short; penalty applies" although delivery continues.
Who acts
Operator.
What the system does automatically
Every 2 s the engine checks that committed hubs hold enough energy above reserve for the rest of the window; delivery moves to other hubs (grant kind substitution). A shortfall held 60 s moves the obligation to SHORTFALL with R-COMMIT-LOCK-INFEASIBLE or an L0/L1/L2 override reason. Hubs a firmware campaign has in flight are excluded like a fault: a shortfall they cause carries R-COMMIT-LOCK-OVERRIDE-L0, and since r3.4.3 the guardian's own read counts them as unavailable so it corroborates that claim (G-19). Decision D-17: it keeps receiving the maximum feasible kW for the rest of its window (never 0, never stopped), the full commitment returns at the earliest feasible interval, and it settles on what was delivered. The guardian re-checks every reduction claim (G-19). No home is discharged below reserve (K1, G-01).

Steps

  1. Note the obligation id from the card. In the header set Ledger to Feeder segment (bank) for the carrying bank and press View; read "Real-time grants & substitutions".
  2. Find the cause: /og/health Alerts and Hub health (comms, see pb-comms-loss); /og/fleet filtered by that bank (SoC, Health, Activity); a SCADA limit (see pb-bank-overload); a firmware campaign on those hubs (pb-firmware).
  3. Read the history in /og/billing "Trace explorer": stream shortfall-<obligation id> (class ALLOCATOR_SHORTFALL) and the obligation's own stream (AT_RISK, AT_RISK_CLEARED, the SHORTFALL transition).
  4. Fix the cause where you can (restore comms, end an operator target that is holding hubs, see pb-manual-target). Restoration of the commitment is automatic: there is no "restore" button or API at r3.4.3.
Confirmations required
None for the automatic path; any manual target you use needs its own two-step confirm.
Verify recovery
The amber border goes, ALR-ENERGY-SHORTFALL-RISK clears (og-engine clears it), grants return to the committed kW, and the trace shows AT_RISK_CLEARED. A measured-delivery AT_RISK is cleared by og-settle's delivery job when delivery recovers (R-DELIVERY-RECOVERED). A SHORTFALL state stays until the window closes, by design.
Escalate when
A firm customer (DIST_DEFERRAL, PARTNER_CAPACITY, ERCOT_AS, tolling) is in SHORTFALL; the energy margin is negative close to the window; ALR-RESERVE-BREACH opens. DOC1 (R2) lists a gap to watch for: a delivery cut by a utility L2 instruction may not return to full when the instruction lifts (watch the grants, not the card).

Measured delivery alert (D-38 delivery verification)

How it shows
Critical alerts, one per call, scope DELIVERY_CALL: ALR-DELIVERY-RAMP-LATE "Call <id> not at target after N s (ramp R s): delivered X kW of Y kW"; ALR-DELIVERY-SHORTFALL "Call <id> below target for N s ..."; ALR-DELIVERY-NONE "Call <id> commanded for N s with no measurable delivery"; and at the end of a call ALR-DELIVERY-METER-MISMATCH "Call <id>: independent meter disagrees with battery telemetry by N%; delivery UNCORROBORATED". While SHORTFALL or NONE is open the obligation's card is AT_RISK (amber). On /og/dispatch the call's row in "AS awards & deployment" shows it in the Delivery column (result badge, a METER badge on a mismatch, delivered / committed kW); a manual discharge target has no row there and is read through the API. See Delivery verification and delivery alerts.
Who acts
Operator; the utility or the QSE desk is told when a firm call runs short.
What the system does automatically
og-settle's delivery job ([delivery] enabled = true, every 15 s, 30 s buckets, 30 s allowance for late telemetry) builds one og.delivery_record (migration 0050) per discharge call: a utility toll call or ERCOT AS deployment (keyed by its deployment id) and a manual discharge target (keyed by its MANUAL_TARGET trace id). It aligns committed kW, commanded kW from guardian-signed batches, delivered kW from hub telemetry (shared by grant) and, on metered banks (every SUBSTATION asset bank, plus [delivery].meter_bank_ids), the SCADA meter against battery telemetry. The ramp time per product is [delivery.ramp_time_s] (ECRS, TOLLING 600 s; NSPIN 1800 s; MANUAL 120 s; unlisted 600 s); target is 95% of committed; SHORTFALL and NONE fire after 60 s. RAMP-LATE, SHORTFALL and NONE clear by themselves when delivery recovers or the call ends; SHORTFALL or NONE also set the obligation AT_RISK (R-DELIVERY-MEASURED-SHORTFALL) and clear it (R-DELIVERY-RECOVERED). METER-MISMATCH is not cleared automatically. The final record is PASS, PARTIAL or FAIL with reasons (RAMP_TOO_SLOW, SUSTAIN_BELOW_TARGET, NO_DELIVERY, VETOED, STOPPED, DATA_STALE, ENERGY_SHORT) and a meter status (CORROBORATED, UNCORROBORATED, NO_METER, METER_STALE), and is traced as DELIVERY_VERIFICATION. It only observes: it never changes a setpoint (K7).

Steps

  1. Note the call id in the alert detail. Open /og/dispatch, find the deployed row in "AS awards & deployment" and click its Delivery value: the Measured delivery drawer shows Result (with reasons), Committed, Time to target, Sustained, Lowest, Energy and Meter, and a chart of Committed, Commanded, Delivered and Meter change kW; Close hides it. For a manual discharge target, or for the vetoed cycles and battery delta, read the record with its per-bucket series: GET /og/api/delivery/records/{call_id} (committed c, commanded m, delivered d, vetoed cycles v, meter and battery deltas md, bd).
  2. Commanded well below committed (the drawer's Commanded line under Committed), or many vetoed cycles: the guardian or the allocator held power back. On a substation toll set, check first for a safe stop on the bank: since r3.4.3 no batch is proposed under an engaged stop, and after the release the set holds 0 kW until fresh telemetry, then ramps again (pb-safe-stop). Read GUARDIAN_VERDICT in the trace explorer for the carrying banks (flow limits, feeder ramp ceilings) and follow pb-veto-burst or pb-bank-overload.
  3. Commanded at target but delivered short: the hubs did not follow. Check /og/fleet for the banks (offline hubs, SoC, faults, a firmware campaign) and pb-comms-loss. DATA_STALE means telemetry, not necessarily delivery, is missing.
  4. Follow the obligation as in pb-at-risk; tell the utility or the desk if a firm call will end short.
  5. METER-MISMATCH (a METER badge in the Delivery column; the drawer's Meter fact shows how far apart): compare the Meter change line with Delivered, or md and bd in the series; pass the bank id, call id and figures to Base field services (meter or battery telemetry calibration). Acknowledge the alert with Ack once the evidence is handed over.
  6. For a trend: GET /og/api/delivery/summary?days=7 (per contract and day: calls, PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL, compliance, meter mismatches, discharged against committed kWh); list failures with GET /og/api/delivery/records?result=FAIL.
Confirmations required
None; the delivery API is read-only (viewer role).
Verify recovery
The live alerts clear, the AT_RISK flag clears, and the final record reads PASS with meter status CORROBORATED (or NO_METER on unmetered banks).
Escalate when
A firm call ends FAIL; ALR-DELIVERY-NONE opens on a running call; meter mismatches repeat on the same bank (a metering problem affects settlement evidence).

Hub or zone comms loss (hubs offline)

How it shows
/og/fleet: the summary counts (Online, Stale, Offline / fault) and the Health column; the hub drawer's "Last communication" and its age badge. A hub turns stale after [health] hub_stale_s (25 s) and offline after hub_offline_s (60 s) without telemetry. The Fleet summary, the age badges, the fleet twin, the Fleet search and the Health rules all use these same values (HealthThresholds from config since r3.4.1), so a hub reporting every 10 s does not flicker stale between reports. /og/health "Hub health" strip; ALR-HUB-OFFLINE-RATIO per zone (warning over 5% offline plus fault, critical over 20%); ALR-SIM-OFFLINE (critical) when no hub telemetry and no SCADA reading has arrived for sim_offline_s (90 s).
Who acts
Operator; admin for the broker or simulators.
What the system does automatically
Hubs hold their last setpoint for the lease plus hold (about 35 s), then serve their own homes (local autonomy). Offline hubs drop out of sellable capacity; delivery is substituted to other hubs, and a loss that cannot be replaced becomes a best-effort SHORTFALL (pb-at-risk); a called obligation also gets a measured-delivery alert (pb-delivery-alert). MQTT clients reconnect by themselves (counter og_mqtt_reconnects_total). Stop messages are retained, so a hub that reconnects still receives an engaged stop.

Steps

  1. /og/fleet: tick Zone and Health (OFFLINE) and press Apply filters; open a hub row for its drawer.
  2. Decide the scale: one hub, one bank, one zone, or the whole fleet. Fleet-wide silence with ALR-SIM-OFFLINE points at the broker, og-engine (it writes telemetry and SCADA readings, so its outage also raises this alert) or the simulators.
  3. Admin: systemctl is-active mosquitto og-engine og-sim-fleet og-sim-scada; journalctl -u og-engine --since -10min. On the simulated fleet, check /ogsim/ for an active comms_loss anomaly (for example scenario demo-03-zone-comms-loss) and stop it.
  4. Follow any affected obligation on /og/dispatch as in pb-at-risk.
Confirmations required
None. A safe stop is not needed for comms loss alone.
Verify recovery
Hubs read online again, the ratio alert clears when the zone is at or below 5%, grants return to the carrying banks.
Escalate when
A zone passes 20% (critical); the whole fleet is silent; the broker does not come back (pb-broker-db); a real field outage is suspected (call Base field services).

Bank overload or SCADA silent

How it shows
ALR-SCADA-OVERLOAD per bank: warning when the latest SCADA apparent power exceeds the bank's kVA rating (600 kVA), critical above 120%. ALR-SCADA-SILENT (critical) when no SCADA bank reading has arrived for scada_silent_s, and ALR-SCADA-SILENT-BANK (warning) per silent bank while others report; the degraded mode SCADA silent (DIST_DEFERRAL_OPEN_LOOP) shows in the bell. Neither is raised before the first reading ever arrives.
Who acts
Operator; the utility (for its own feeder); admin for the SCADA link or simulator.
What the system does automatically
Overload: the DIST_DEFERRAL grant on the bank rises (more discharge relieves the segment) while committed kW stay unchanged; the guardian's flow checks (G-26 to G-33) bound every setpoint; a utility LIMIT instruction is applied by og-engine. Since D-36 (r3.4.1) each 600 kVA home bank has 12 service transformers of 50 kVA whose ratings sum to the bank rating. SCADA silent: shown and recorded only. Nothing acts on it at r3.4.3; the DIST_DEFERRAL loop keeps using the last SCADA reading.

Steps

  1. /og/health, Alerts: filter Rule ALR-SCADA-OVERLOAD (or ALR-SCADA-SILENT); note the bank and the percentage in the summary.
  2. /og/dispatch: Ledger Feeder segment (bank), that bank, View; check the DIST_DEFERRAL grant and the committed cards.
  3. If the overload persists at critical, call the utility. A safe stop on the bank removes OpenGrid's dispatch support there; it does not reduce the bank's own load, so use it only for a device or safety concern (pb-safe-stop).
  4. SCADA silent, admin: check og-sim-scada (simulated SCADA) or the real SCADA link (RB-023 intent). Treat DIST_DEFERRAL decisions as open loop until readings return.
Confirmations required
None, unless you engage a safe stop.
Verify recovery
The overload alert clears when the reading is at or under the rating; SCADA silent clears when a reading arrives (og-settle clears it), and the bell's "Degraded mode: SCADA silent" disappears.
Escalate when
Critical overload for more than a few minutes; ALR-BANK-UNMAPPED-TOPOLOGY is open (the bank has no feeder, so the guardian cannot run G-06/G-28/G-32 for it; with [guardian.flow] fail_closed_missing_topology = true it vetoes any batch that raises that bank's net setpoint, BANK_TOPOLOGY_UNMAPPED) or ALR-SUBSTATION-UNMAPPED-TOPOLOGY is open (no substation mapping or limit row, so G-29 is skipped for the bank): fix the mapping through a topology re-seed (deploy/scripts/topology_backfill.sh, dry run by default); SCADA silent during a DIST_DEFERRAL delivery window; the utility issues a LIMIT with no expiry.

Regulated zone unavailable: "Regulated market – no contract" (LZ_LCRA, LZ_RAYBN, D-37)

How it shows
Only where the zones are enabled (bootstrap --noie-blocks; production runs LZ_AEN only). On /og/fleet hubs of LZ_LCRA (bank-050 to bank-059) and LZ_RAYBN (bank-060 to bank-069) carry the badge "Regulated market – no contract" with the tooltip "Unavailable: regulated (NOIE) territory, so energy can't be sold into ERCOT, and there is no utility capacity contract to reserve it. Available once a contract is signed."; the drawer repeats it; the map draws hollow dashed markers; the "Availability" filter lists them. Each utility has a contract labelled SAMPLE - INACTIVE ("Sample Contract: ...").
Who acts
Operator explains; the owner decides; the release manager or lead flips a zone once a real contract is signed.

The banks are og.bank.availability = UNAVAILABLE, reason REGULATED_NO_CONTRACT (migration 0046, dev/seed/noie_switch_seed.sql, applied to production by deploy/scripts/noie_switch_apply.py). Nothing is offered, planned or dispatched there and they are not charged (idle hold); the guardian vetoes any non-idle item (G-33, R-BANK-UNAVAILABLE-REGULATED-NO-CONTRACT).   1. Someone asks why capacity is missing: point to the badge and the separate capacity line; this is expected, not a fault.
  2. A manual target was refused with the badge text: expected. A 0 kW hold and a safe stop are allowed.
  3. Check grandfathered ERCOT obligations: deploy/scripts/noie_switch_check.sql (read-only).
  4. Making a zone AVAILABLE once a real contract is signed (release manager or lead, owner's approval; operator guide 6.10): enter the real contract for the utility (market = 'REGULATED', is_sample = false) and set it ACTIVE; then, in one transaction, set that utility's banks availability = 'AVAILABLE', availability_reason = NULL. Nothing to restart. To let the utility call through the API, add it to [api.utility_api].enabled_utilities and create its Apache account through a release.
Confirmations required
The owner's approval and the lead for any flip to AVAILABLE; record it in the decision log.
Verify recovery
After a flip: the badge is gone on /og/fleet, the capacity moves into available kW, and at the next gate the utility's toll window is reserved.
Escalate when
Any bank outside LZ_LCRA/LZ_RAYBN shows the badge; anything is dispatched or charged on an UNAVAILABLE bank (a G-33 breach); a sample contract appears ACTIVE or billed.

Guardian veto burst: K7 conservative, then a safe-stop request

How it shows
ALR-SCOPE-CONSERVATIVE (warning): the bell's Safety group reads "N scope(s) held conservative" and links to the Health alerts filtered on that rule. Then ALR-SAFE-STOP-REQUESTED (critical): the Safety group lists "Guardian requests a safe stop: bank bank-007" with a Review (two-step) button, and the critical strip under the header shows it with a View link. The hub drawer's "Verdict" shows the last command's outcome (for example VETOED).
Who acts
Operator decides; a second operator only if a stop is later released.
What the system does automatically
Per bank and per zone, every 2 s tick: more than 5% of a scope's commands vetoed puts it CONSERVATIVE (the engine stops selling spot headroom there; committed deliveries continue). An escalation count of 3 raises the stop request and records an unconfirmed safe-stop proposal. The guardian never engages a stop itself (K8). The scope returns to normal after 3 consecutive good ticks (at most 2.5% vetoed) or 30 ticks with no commands; the count then drops by one per good tick. The posture lives in og-guardian's memory, so after a guardian restart a scope may stay CONSERVATIVE until it recovers again. If the guardian does not answer in time, og-engine does not wait: since r3.4.3 the whole propose phase is bounded ([allocator].propose_timeout_s, 2 s), banks still pending are reported as timed out, and hubs hold their last signed command.

Steps

  1. Open the bell; note the scope. On /og/fleet, filter by that bank or zone and open a few hub drawers (Verdict, Fault, Last communication).
  2. Find the rule ids: /og/billing "Trace explorer", class GUARDIAN_VERDICT. Typical causes are bad telemetry, a flow limit (G-26 to G-33), an unavailable regulated bank (G-33, pb-regulated-unavailable), or an operator target pushing hubs; cancel such a target with Cancel target (pb-manual-target).
  3. If the cause is a device or area fault that needs dispatch stopped: press Review (two-step). It opens /og/fleet with the "Safe stop" card prefilled (scope, scope id, reason "Guardian escalation: safe stop requested") and the note "Prefilled from the guardian's safe-stop request...". Continue with pb-safe-stop.
  4. If not, do nothing: with the cause gone the escalation clears on its own.
Confirmations required
Only if you stop: propose, then confirm within 30 s.
Verify recovery
Both alerts clear (og-guardian clears them) and the Safety group empties. The guardian's unconfirmed proposal stays in the operator-action log and is never acted on.
Escalate when
Stop requests recur on the same scope; ALR-CLOCK-QUALITY is open (every verdict is TIMEOUT while the clock is off, G-20); vetoes spread across zones. Note: ALR-GUARDIAN-TIMEOUT-RATE is never raised with the shipped config ([health] guardian_metrics_url is not set).

DEMO-2 step 15 triggers the burst with a loop of POST /og/api/fleet/command confirms expecting 409 vetoes. Since r3.4 a confirmed manual command returns 202 RAMPING and the guardian judges each engine step instead, so that loop no longer shows a row of 409s.

Emergency safe stop and the two-person release

How it shows
Engaged: the result badge ENGAGED "Safe stop engaged for bank/bank-022. Trace ..."; the bank's hubs drop toward 0 kW on /og/fleet. Pending releases appear in the "Release request id" list.
Who acts
Any operator engages. Release: operator 1 requests, a different authorised operator (today og-op-a / og-op-b) approves.
What the system does automatically
og-safestop signs the stop with its own stop-only key and publishes a retained message per scope (og/v1/stop/<scope>/<id>/<stop_id>, event og.stop_event). While a bank is stopped the guardian signs no command for it, so its hubs get no new setpoint; since r3.4.3 og-engine also proposes no batch for a bank under an engaged FLEET, ZONE or BANK stop, so the stop does not produce a stream of vetoes. A stop never forces a home below reserve. og-safestop works with og-engine and og-guardian down; it needs og-api, Postgres and Mosquitto. Every accepted ENGAGE or RELEASE is written together with its stop outbox entry (og.stop_outbox) in one transaction and republished until the broker acknowledges it, including after a broker outage. Publish order (r3.4.1): within one scope entries go strictly in acceptance order, so an ENGAGE never overtakes an older RELEASE of the same scope; across scopes the oldest ENGAGE at the head of any scope goes before any RELEASE, so a stop never waits behind another scope's release. Since r3.4.3 each drain reads the oldest 100 entries plus every entry of each scope with a queued ENGAGE, so a stop never waits behind a backlog, and a permanently failing entry holds back only its scope's later RELEASEs, never an ENGAGE. og-safestop refuses a guardian-signed RELEASE whose signed issued_at is older than the newest ENGAGE on its scope (the hubs would drop it): nothing is published or recorded, the scope stays stopped, a SAFE_STOP trace (reason SAFE_STOP_RELEASE_SUPERSEDED) marks it superseded, ALR-STOP-RELEASE-SUPERSEDED (warning) is raised once, and the guardian no longer re-hands it. A redelivered utility instruction whose stop was recorded but never queued is re-queued under the same stop id. An entry that fails permanently 5 times is dead-lettered: skipped, traced, and ALR-STOP-PUBLISH-DEAD-LETTER (critical) is raised once, so later stops still go out; a periodic sweep raises it for dead letters left behind and traces each newly raised one. Since r3.4.3 a dead-lettered ENGAGE is still retried on every drain after the live queue and traced DEAD_LETTER_PUBLISHED when it finally goes out; a dead-lettered RELEASE is only alerted. Engaging a stop also cancels every manual target it covers (status CANCELLED_BY_SAFE_STOP), including a stop engaged more than 24 h ago and never released; the target does not resume after the release. The guardian signs a release only after its own checks; og-safestop's key can never sign one. og-safestop's metrics are on localhost:9106/metrics.

Steps: engage

  1. /og/fleet, "Actions", card "Safe stop": Scope (Fleet, Zone, Bank), Scope id (empty for Fleet), Reason.
  2. Press Propose safe stop (step 1 of 2). The dialog "Confirm scoped safe stop" shows the API's summary and "Expires in 30s".
  3. Press Engage safe stop within the countdown (focus starts on Cancel; Tab moves to the confirm button). Results: ENGAGED, TIMEOUT (no confirmation from og-safestop), EXPIRED (propose again), FAILED.
  4. API: POST /og/api/safestop with {"scope": "bank", "scope_id": "bank-034", "reason": "..."}, then POST /og/api/safestop/{proposal_id}/confirm within 30 s (later answers 409 "proposal expired, propose again").

Steps: release (two people)

  1. Operator 1, card "Release a safe stop": Scope, Scope id, Reason, Request release (operator 1). Result REQUESTED with the request id and "a second operator approves it below within 60 s".
  2. Operator 2 (different account): pick the request in "Release request id" (it lists id, scope, requester and age), press Review and approve (operator 2), read "Approve safe-stop release", press Approve release.
  3. Results: RELEASED "Safe stop released for ..."; PENDING (approved, the guardian has not signed within og-api's 10 s wait); REFUSED "The requesting operator cannot approve their own release." (the request stays valid for another operator); EXPIRED; FAILED.
  4. API: POST /og/api/safestop/{scope}/{scope_id}/release (operator 1), POST /og/api/safestop/release/{proposal_id}/approve (operator 2: 200 released, 202 pending, 403 same person, 410 expired).
Confirmations required
Engage: two steps by one operator, confirm within 30 s ([safestop].confirm_window_s). Release: two different authorised operators; the approval must be fresh (at most 300 s).
Verify recovery
After release the bank's hubs take commands again (drawer "Last command" changes; P follows the allocator). A utility-scale set (the 20 MW substation battery) first holds 0 kW until a telemetry report newer than the release arrives (about 10 s), then ramps from its measured power at one cycle's G-04 step; a toll call still running resumes without a run of G-04 vetoes (r3.4.3). The trace shows SAFE_STOP_ENGAGE, SAFE_STOP_RELEASE (on the approver's stream, with the requester in the payload) and the guardian's signed release (GUARDIAN_VERDICT).
Escalate when
The guardian refuses the release (GUARDIAN_VERDICT kind=STOP_RELEASE outcome=REFUSED: unauthorised operator, same person twice, stale approval, no stop engaged, a stop engaged after the approval, a utility-initiated stop, or an active ESTOP/BLOCK in scope): the stop stays engaged; fix the cause and request again. ALR-STOP-RELEASE-SUPERSEDED is open ("release of <scope> superseded by a newer stop; re-issue the two-person release"): a newer ENGAGE on the same scope arrived before the signed release reached og-safestop, so the release was refused and the scope is still stopped. If the scope should be released, run the two-person release again; the guardian signs the new one after the ENGAGE and it is relayed normally. The alert does not clear itself; acknowledge it once the new release is through. If og-api is down no stop can be requested from the console or API; the admin restores og-api first (systemctl restart og-api). ALR-STOP-PUBLISH-DEAD-LETTER is open: that stop message was never published, so the hubs did not receive it. Read the entry's last_error in og.stop_outbox and journalctl -u og-safestop, fix the cause, then engage the stop again from Fleet (it gets a new stop id; recommended practice, the system does not do this automatically) and tell the lead.

Manual command or target: ramp, expiry, cancel

How it shows
After confirm: badge RAMPING "Ramping N hubs to X kW ... Holds until ... Trace ...", a progress bar and "Now: ..."; the Hubs table bar reads "N under an operator target" and each row "target X kW"; the hub drawer shows "Operator target X kW until ... (by ...: reason)".
Who acts
Operator.
What the system does automatically
Confirm records a MANUAL_TARGET trace event (reason code MANUAL_OPERATOR) on its own stream manual_target:<user>, through a database-or-nothing write that is never put in the local journal. The confirm succeeds only once that event is in og.trace. If the write fails, og-api looks the row up by the request's id: found means success (202); absent means 503 "manual target not recorded ... nothing will ramp -- retry" (true: it can never appear later by journal replay or restart); if the lookup itself fails, 503 "manual target outcome unknown ... it may be live -- check GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets before retrying". A failure to write the operator-action row after the target is durable returns 202 with a warning. A non-zero target on a hub of an UNAVAILABLE regulated bank is refused with 409 (pb-regulated-unavailable). og-engine then ramps each hub toward the target at the hub's ramp limit every cycle (G-04), every step signed by the guardian; the guardian accepts the override only from its own read of live targets (G-19, R-OPERATOR-OVERRIDE). Engine, API and guardian share one status rule (core.manual_targets.effective_targets): ACTIVE, EXPIRED, CANCELLED_BY_OPERATOR, CANCELLED_BY_SAFE_STOP (a covering stop engaged at or after the target, or still engaged) or CANCELLED_LATE_RECORD (the target reached the trace more than 30 s after it was issued; it is never activated). Only ACTIVE targets show as markers and ramps, and the ramp display stops when the target ends. At expires_at (1 to 240 min, default 15) the hubs return to normal dispatch on the next cycle. Two steps within 60 s; a selection that overrides customer-serving or faulted hubs needs the acknowledgement tick and may need a second confirm.
Verify recovery

Firmware rollout: staged, halted, retry, rollback

How it shows
/og/fleet: the "FW version" and "HW rev" columns and filters, and "FW ≠ (out of date)"; the drawer's "HW rev" and "FW". Alerts ALR-FIRMWARE-CAMPAIGN-STARTED and ALR-FIRMWARE-CAMPAIGN-COMPLETED (info), ALR-FIRMWARE-HUB-FAILED (warning, one per hub failed for good), ALR-FIRMWARE-CAMPAIGN-HALTED (critical). Since r3.4.3 open info-severity alerts no longer break the alert lists (before, one open info alert made GET /og/api/alerts and the health auto-clear fail).
Who acts
Operator proposes and confirms; a second operator approves when required.
What the system does automatically
og-engine's executor works through the waves (default canary of 1 hub, then 25% steps; next wave only when the current one has finished; a canary with no success halts). Caps: at most 10% of a bank's and of a feeder's hubs at once. Halt at 2 terminal failures or 5%. Every attempt is signed by the guardian after G-36 (campaign RUNNING, hub online and not stopped, not serving a committed obligation in the next 15 min, SoC at least reserve plus 10%, catalogued image and sha256 for its hardware, caps). A refusal is a hold, re-checked after 60 s. Retries: up to 3 attempts with backoff for no ack, silence or download errors; never for a sha256 mismatch, incompatibility, bad signature or install failure. A hub with an update in flight is excluded from dispatch like a fault; delivery is substituted, and since r3.4.3 the guardian also counts it as unavailable when it checks a reduction claim (G-19, R-COMMIT-LOCK-OVERRIDE-L0).

Steps

  1. Not possible in the UI at r3.4.3: the Fleet card "Firmware update" shows a disabled Firmware update — available after R3.1 button, although the API is served. Use the API as an operator.
  2. Check the catalogue: GET /og/api/firmware/catalogue. The r3.4.3 catalogue is the demo set for the simulated fleet (placeholder sha256 values); never use it for real batteries.
  3. Propose: POST /og/api/firmware/campaigns with name, target_version, selection (hub ids or banks, zones, feeders, hardware revisions, current versions), waves, caps, optional window, reason. Hubs already on the version, or with no catalogue entry, are SKIPPED.
  4. Confirm within 60 s: POST /og/api/firmware/campaigns/{id}/confirm.
  5. If requires_second_operator (more than 50 hubs, override_committed, or allow_downgrade): a different operator calls POST .../{id}/approve.
  6. Watch: GET .../campaigns/{id} (per-hub state and counts) and GET .../campaigns/{id}/events?after=<event_id>.
  7. Pause, resume or abort: POST .../{id}/pause, /resume, /abort (a hub mid-install cannot be recalled).
  8. Halted: read the failures first (RB-005: compare the failed cohort with the control cohort). Retry: POST .../{id}/retry-failed, then .../retry-failed/{proposal_id}/confirm; the campaign becomes PAUSED, resume it deliberately. Roll back a hub: POST .../{id}/hubs/{hub_id}/rollback, then .../rollback/{proposal_id}/confirm.
Confirmations required
Propose plus confirm within 60 s; second operator (never the proposer or confirmer) when flagged; retry and rollback are two-step.
Verify recovery
ALR-FIRMWARE-CAMPAIGN-COMPLETED; the FW version filter shows the target on the selected hubs; no open ALR-FIRMWARE-HUB-FAILED.
Escalate when
ALR-FIRMWARE-CAMPAIGN-HALTED; a canary fails; failures cluster on one hardware revision (suspect image: roll back affected hubs).

Broker or database outage: reconnect, journal, stop outbox

How it shows
Broker down: telemetry stops, so ALR-SIM-OFFLINE and ALR-HUB-OFFLINE-RATIO open and live badges read reconnecting. Database down: console pages and the API fail; no health alert is raised or cleared (the evaluator in og-settle needs Postgres) and the degraded modes freeze. After recovery, ALR-TRACE-QUARANTINED (critical) may appear if a journaled trace row was refused by Postgres for its content.
Who acts
Admin, with the lead; operators watch.
What the system does automatically
Hubs hold about 35 s, then serve their homes. MQTT clients reconnect on their own (og_mqtt_reconnects_total). og-safestop records every accepted ENGAGE or RELEASE together with its og.stop_outbox entry in one transaction and republishes them once the broker is back, in the r3.4.1 order (per scope in acceptance order; across scopes an ENGAGE first; pb-safe-stop); a broker outage never counts toward the dead-letter limit. Trace writes fall back to the local append-only journal [trace].journal_path (/var/lib/opengrid/trace_journal.jsonl, fsynced per line off the event loop) and drain into Postgres automatically when it returns (K11). Since r3.4.1 a row Postgres refuses for its content (for example invalid characters) is never retried: it is moved to trace_journal.jsonl.quarantine.jsonl next to the journal, traced once as TRACE_QUARANTINED on stream trace_quarantine (identifiers only) and raises ALR-TRACE-QUARANTINED, and the replay continues instead of stopping. Manual targets never go to the journal: while the database is down a confirm or cancel answers 503 (NOT RECORDED, or "outcome unknown" when og-api cannot re-check) and nothing ramps. A database stall no longer freezes og-engine: the propose phase is bounded at 2 s and the guardian heartbeat read at 0.5 s ([allocator].propose_timeout_s, guardian_check_timeout_s); after a timeout the guardian counts as unavailable until it writes a newer heartbeat, and hubs hold their last signed command (r3.4.3).

Steps (admin, root; deploy/RUNBOOK.md)

  1. Status: systemctl is-active mosquitto postgresql@17-main; journalctl -u mosquitto or the Postgres log /var/log/postgresql/postgresql-17-main.log.
  2. Restart by exact unit name (never pkill): systemctl restart mosquitto or systemctl start postgresql@17-main; then the og units if they exited (systemctl start opengrid.target ogsim.target).
  3. Postgres data directory suspect (I/O errors, will not start): follow deploy/ha/FAILOVER.md (manual promotion of the hot standby on port 5433, repoint [postgres], verify the trace chain), or restore the latest dump from /srv/ogbackup into a scratch database first (RUNBOOK "Backup and restore").
  4. After recovery: POST /og/api/trace/verify; confirm the trace journal has drained; check that every stop engaged during the outage reached its hubs (/og/fleet, P near 0 kW on the stopped scope) and that no ALR-STOP-PUBLISH-DEAD-LETTER is open; re-propose any manual target whose confirm answered "not recorded", and look up any "outcome unknown" one with GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets first.
  5. ALR-TRACE-QUARANTINED open: read the error in the quarantine file (identifiers and error only; do not paste payloads into tickets), find the writer from the stream id, and tell the lead. Do not edit or re-insert the row by hand.
Confirmations required
The lead's OK before a failover or a restore (both are destructive if done out of order).
Verify recovery
All units active; curl -fsS http://localhost:8080/og/api/health succeeds; heartbeats current on /og/health; alerts clear; trace verify passed: true.
Escalate when
The trace chain does not verify after a promotion or restore (stop and involve the lead before og-engine writes new rows); the journal does not drain; ALR-TRACE-QUARANTINED repeats; the broker keeps dropping clients (RB-015 intent).

None in the console; follow the RUNBOOK caution on restarts during delivery.

Admin / release manager, with the lead's OK; the operator on shift is told first.
What the system does automatically
deploy.sh refuses (exit 3) while a FIRM or AS obligation (ERCOT_AS, DIST_DEFERRAL, PARTNER_CAPACITY, DATA_CENTER) is DELIVERING unless given --during-delivery; copies the release, runs forward-only migrations, re-seeds topology (idempotent, from /etc/opengrid/sim/fleet.yaml), switches current, reinstalls the release's systemd units (timers installed, never enabled), creates the runtime directories, restarts both targets, then checks that every og-* unit is active with a fresh heartbeat and polls /og/api/health for 30 s, rolling back automatically on any failure. It keeps the 5 newest releases.

Steps (RUNBOOK "Deploy a release" and "Rollback")

  1. Check /og/dispatch for DELIVERING firm or AS cards and running utility calls; pick a quiet moment (right after a Postgres checkpoint), outside the tolling window.
  2. In /opt/opengrid/src: git pull --ff-only as opengrid, git archive HEAD into /root/release-<rev>, install the release's deploy.sh, run bash /opt/opengrid/deploy/scripts/deploy.sh /root/release-<rev>.
  3. If a yaml under integration-sims/config changed, regenerate /etc/opengrid/sim/fleet.yaml and scada.yaml before the deploy, because the seed reads them: python deploy/scripts/gen_sim_overrides.py --release <release_dir> --out /etc/opengrid/sim --zones LZ_AEN (the owner-approved zones; see Releases).
  4. Release-specific scripts are dry run by default (APPLY=1 or --apply to apply). For r3.4.3 run, before deploy.sh: r341_utility_api_apache.sh (utility API account and utility_sim.env), then r343_utility_sim_install.sh (MQTT user, og-sim-utility); without them og-sim-utility cannot start, the post-restart check fails and the deploy rolls back. Data scripts (noie_switch_apply.py then topology_backfill.sh, D-37 and D-36) run after the deploy. Follow the release notes.
  5. Rollback: ls -1t /opt/opengrid/releases, then bash /opt/opengrid/deploy/scripts/rollback.sh <timestamp>. Migrations are not reverted; data-only migrations must be reversed by hand if needed.
Confirmations required
The lead's OK; --during-delivery only as an explicit decision (a restart interrupts delivery for about 35 s).
Verify recovery
All units active; the console loads for operator and viewer; guardian verdicts mostly PASS; og.invariant_check shows no new violations; engine p99 normal; no ALR-PROCESS-DOWN.
Escalate when
The automatic rollback fired; a migration failed; the health check passes but the guardian vetoes most batches.

From-scratch rebuild

How it shows
The host or its disks are lost, or a clean re-install is required.
Who acts
Admin / release manager with the lead.
What the system does automatically
One idempotent script, deploy/scripts/bootstrap_from_scratch.sh, rebuilds the whole solution in phases a-l: a prerequisites check; b OS user and directories (existing directory modes are kept); c and d deploy/scripts/create_schema.sh (role, database, schema og, then migrations 0001 to the latest, 0050 at r3.4.3, through the one migration runner); e the seeds in order (fleet, market model, customer services, services, trucks, topology) with the count check bootstrap_check.py; f sim configs; g MQTT users and ACL (render_mosquitto_acl.sh); h the Ed25519 keys, each private key in its own group readable only by the unit that signs with it (og-guardian-key, og-safestop-key, og-anchor-key); i proxy secret and accounts (the secret is never on a command line); j systemd units; k optional ERCOT backfill; l first deploy and post-start checks. Every phase converges; existing secrets, keys and accounts are kept, never rotated or printed. create_schema.sh can also check the schema against the snapshot orchestrator/schema/og_schema.sql (--check-snapshot). Details: Bootstrap from scratch and deploy/BOOTSTRAP.md.

Steps

  1. By hand: storage (/srv/pgdata, /srv/pgstandby, /srv/ogbackup), the packages and the two venvs (deploy/BOOTSTRAP.md steps 1-2). The owner places /etc/opengrid/api_keys.env and, optionally, ai_agent.env; never copy secrets or keys through chat or tickets.
  2. Get the release: git archive <tag> | tar -x -C /root/release-<tag>. From there, as root: bash deploy/scripts/bootstrap_from_scratch.sh --dry-run, read what each phase would do, then run it without --dry-run. Use --phase to repeat single phases (for example --phase k once the ERCOT keys are installed). Production zones default to LZ_AEN; add --noie-blocks (alias --d32, deprecated) only when the owner enables LZ_LCRA and LZ_RAYBN in production (regulated, unavailable until a contract is signed, D-37). On a host whose keys are still in the old opengrid 0600 layout, --migrate-key-perms moves them to the per-key groups (then restart og-guardian, og-safestop and og-settle).
  3. To prove a release seeds correctly without touching production, first run make bootstrap-check (phases c-e into a fresh og_t_boot on the test cluster, port 5433).
  4. Restore the latest dump if data must survive: restore into og_restore_check, verify, then rename (RUNBOOK "Backup and restore"); run POST /og/api/trace/verify.
  5. Enable og-lifecycle.timer after the I/O check (Data lifecycle); replace the test release operators with named accounts; re-run the release scripts the production host needs (utility API account, r343_utility_sim_install.sh); stand up the hot standby again if used (deploy/ha/pg_standby_setup.sh). For a local copy only: make dev-up, or make dev-reset for a full wipe (dev/README.md).
  6. On a Kubernetes cluster instead of a base server: deploy/k8s/install.sh (phases prereqs, images, namespace, secrets, deploy, verify; --dry-run renders the chart). It reuses create_schema.sh and bootstrap_from_scratch.sh --phase e. At r3.4.3 it is validated offline only and has never been deployed to a real cluster; the chart has no objects yet for og-sim-utility and some r3.4.2/r3.4.3 additions (deploy/k8s/README.md). Treat a first install as a pilot.
Confirmations required
The lead's OK at each destructive step (--fresh-db is refused on port 5432).
Verify recovery
The script's phase l checks: 10 units active, /og/api/health 200, /og/ 401 without credentials, every hub fresh within 25 s, invariants 0, every topology unmapped count 0, only forecast degraded modes until price history exists. Then as after a deploy, plus trace verify on restored data and a working two-person release with named accounts.
Escalate when
bootstrap_check.py reports a count mismatch; create_schema.sh --check-snapshot reports drift; the restored trace chain fails verification; signing keys had to be regenerated (hubs and og-safestop must trust the new guardian key, see pb-security).

Security event: a denied action, key or secret rotation

How it shows
A write answered 403 (for example "Operator role required to acknowledge alerts."); a guardian refusal (GUARDIAN_VERDICT outcome REFUSED, reason such as OPERATOR_NOT_AUTHORISED); an AUTHZ_DENY trace (a utility reaching for another utility's obligation, answered 404; a refused grid-link peer, GRID_LINK_DENY); the banner "... no identity reached the console" when og-api is reached directly on its port; ALR-TRACE-VERIFY-FAILED, ALR-ANCHOR-PUBLISH-FAILED, ALR-TRACE-VERDICT-WRITE-FAILED.
Who acts
Operator reports; admin and lead investigate and rotate.
What the system does automatically
og-api believes an identity only with Apache's proxy secret; unknown accounts are viewers; every operator action is traced on operator_action:<user> (calls on dispatch_call:<principal>); hubs execute only guardian-signed commands for their current lease epoch (K3, K6); og-safestop's key can only stop; since r3.4.1 each private key is readable only by the unit that signs with it; the trace is hash-chained and checked every 5 minutes.

Steps

  1. Denied action: confirm who and what in /og/billing "Trace explorer" (filter the class, e.g. GUARDIAN_VERDICT) and in journalctl -u og-api. A legitimate user with the wrong role: fix the account (pb-personas). An unknown user or repeated attempts: tell the lead.
  2. Password rotation: htpasswd -B /etc/opengrid/htpasswd <user>; update the credentials file by hand; htpasswd -D removes an account (also remove it from [api.roles], stop_release_authorised_operators and the Apache Require user line; deploy; apache2ctl configtest before systemctl reload apache2).
  3. Service secrets (secrets.env, api_keys.env, ai_agent.env, utility_sim.env): edit on the server, restart the units that read them. Never print them.
  4. Signing keys (guardian, safe-stop, trace anchor) and grid-link certificates: no rotation procedure is implemented at r3.4.3. RB-057 describes the intent; a guardian key change also needs every verifier (hubs, og-safestop) to trust the new public key. Treat as a lead-owned change.
  5. Chain failure: run POST /og/api/trace/verify; note first_broken stream and sequence; do not edit rows.
Confirmations required
The lead for any account, secret or key change.
Verify recovery
The affected user can do exactly what the role allows; trace verify passed: true; no anchor alerts.
Escalate when
Any sign of a forged or unsigned command, a broken chain, a leaked secret or key, or a spoofed identity header: immediately.

Customer onboarding (customer and utility API)

How it shows
A new customer, utility or contract; its opportunities then appear as cards on /og/dispatch.
Who acts
Operator (contract admission, API-only); admin (accounts and config); the owner decides go-live.
What the system does automatically
Admission checks every contract and opportunity; the selector commits only what fits. At r3.4.3 [api.customer_api] enabled = true (D-33, for the Austin Energy utility API), so every customer route is mounted, each scoped to the caller's own customer_id or utility_id. Still held back: [contracts.activation] data_center = false (DATA_CENTER and PIPELINE_AC admission closed, R-ADMIT-REJECT); [allocator.closed_loop] enabled = false and [site_ingest] enabled = false; the og-sim-customer unit is installed but not enabled.

Steps

  1. Not possible in the UI at r3.4.3 (no contract screen). As an operator: GET /og/api/customers, POST /og/api/contracts, PATCH /og/api/contracts/{contract_id}, GET /og/api/contracts; offer an opportunity with POST /og/api/opportunities.
  2. Admin, a customer: create its Apache account, add it to the <Location /og/api/customer/> Require user line and map it to its customer_id in [api.roles.customer]; deploy. The customer then uses /og/api/customer/...; operators handle disputes and requests with GET/PATCH /og/api/customer-disputes and /og/api/customer-requests.
  3. Admin, a utility: map its account to the utility_id in [api.roles.utility], add the utility to [api.utility_api].enabled_utilities only once it has an ACTIVE, non-sample tolling contract, and create the Apache account (as r341_utility_api_apache.sh does for og-util-aen). See Utility API.
  4. For closed-loop services (DATA_CENTER, PIPELINE_AC), the activation, closed-loop and site-ingest switches are separate owner decisions.
Confirmations required
The owner for any go-live (a new utility, closed-loop services); the lead for account and config changes.
Verify recovery
The contract lists in GET /og/api/contracts; its first opportunity moves through the pipeline; GET /og/api/customer/me (customer) or GET /og/api/customer/v1/utility/me (utility) answers for the new account.
Escalate when
Admission rejects a contract you expect to pass; anyone asks to enable a utility or a closed-loop service without the owner's confirmation.
