How it works

Decision cycle

OpenGrid decides on two time scales. Every 15 minutes the selector decides what to sell and commit to over the next day. Every 2 seconds the allocator decides how much each bank delivers right now against what is already committed. The guardian checks and signs every resulting command before any battery moves.

Price extremes (FR-ING-117, r3.4.1, migration 0045). A real-time price outside the normal band (-$250 to $5,000/MWh) is kept, never clipped, and stored with quality EXTREME_UNCORROBORATED; it becomes GOOD once the same value (to the cent) is posted for the preceding 15-minute interval too. A value outside the hard bounds (-$10,000 to $50,000/MWh) or not a number is quarantined and never stored. While the latest price of a series is uncorroborated, its forecast is flagged NOT_FOR_FIRM and the selector withholds firm candidates priced off it.

At r3.4.3 og-feeds polls only the ERCOT products listed in [feeds.ercot].products (all six above in the shipped config; an unknown name stops og-feeds at startup, and disabled_products can still remove a listed one). A failed poll, or one skipped because the circuit breaker is open, is retried after 5 minutes, doubling per consecutive failure up to 60 minutes and never later than the product's normal cadence, so one missed daily np4-188-cd poll no longer leaves AS prices stale for a day.

og-feeds recomputes the forecast every 15 minutes: a 24-hour horizon at 15-minute resolution with quantiles 0.10, 0.50 and 0.90 (P10/P50/P90), for eight load-zone price and load series ([forecast]). A stale feed that is allowed to block firm commitments puts the system into NO_NEW_COMMITMENTS: existing commitments keep running, new ones are not made.

The 15-minute selector gate

  • og-engine checks the wall-clock 15-minute gate on each 2 s tick and runs selector.run_gate; it also runs gates for due ADMISSION and RENOMINATION triggers. Gate kinds in og.plan: SCHEDULED_15MIN, ADMISSION, RENOMINATION.
  • The model is a mixed-integer linear program (MILP), solved with HiGHS, over banks x 96 intervals x P10/P50/P90 scenarios. Existing commitments are loaded frozen (K13, constraint C24). Regulated candidates are selected first (stage R), then the full net value on the rest (stage F).
  • Time limits: 45 s for the scheduled gate, 15 s for admission and re-nomination, target relative gap 1 %.
  • The solution is re-validated independently (K1, K2, K13, product rules). On infeasibility, a time-out without an acceptable gap, or a failed validation, the deterministic rule selector F2 is used instead (plan_mode RULE_FALLBACK). F2 also runs on every gate as the comparison baseline.
  • Each bank is priced at its own load zone. Wear is charged at [allocator].wear_usd_per_kwh = 0.03 on discharged kWh; charging cost includes M1 in the ERCOT competitive area (see Market model).
  • A failed gate is traced and raises ALR-SELECTOR-GATE-FAILED; the allocator keeps serving what is already committed.

The 2-second engine and allocator cycle

  1. Write the engine heartbeat (og.heartbeat).
  2. Start the obligation lifecycle step (COMMITTED to DELIVERING, window-end FULFILLED/SHORTFALL, expiry of unselected offers) in the background (r3.4.3): single-flight, so a pass still running is skipped, never queued, and abandoned after 30 s; the tick does not wait for it. Each state change and its trace row are written together or not at all.
  3. Flush the fleet twin (latest hub telemetry and health).
  4. Run any due selector gate (above).
  5. Run the allocator ([allocator].cycle_interval_s = 2; idle banks every 10 s). For every bank with an active obligation it re-derives how much of each committed kW is deliverable now, substitutes hubs within an obligation on health loss, applies the DIST_DEFERRAL PI loop, enforces flow limits and territory, then offers leftover headroom to the ERCOT energy market with a 300 s dwell (dwell_s) and a $5/MWh hysteresis (hysteresis_usd_per_mwh).
  6. If the guardian's heartbeat is current, write one og.command_batch per bank with grants and NOTIFY og_command_batch. If not, the cycle holds and no new batches are proposed. At r3.4.3 this hand-off is bounded: the heartbeat read gives up after [allocator].guardian_check_timeout_s (default 0.5 s, a timeout means unavailable) and the whole propose phase after [allocator].propose_timeout_s (default 2 s). Banks still pending are cut off and reported as timed out while the rest go out (K7); the guardian then counts as unavailable, and the next cycles hold, until it writes a newer heartbeat. Hubs keep their last signed command meanwhile.
  7. The guardian evaluates each batch on its own reads and returns PASS, PARTLY_VETOED, VETOED or TIMEOUT (og.verdict). Passed items are signed and published; a vetoed hub is excluded and re-solved next cycle (R-HUB-VETO-EXCLUDED).

Safe stop and the engine (r3.4.3): og-engine proposes no batch at all for a bank covered by an engaged og.stop_event (FLEET, its ZONE or its BANK), on the tick and in the K4 re-propose (core.manual_targets.stop_covers). Utility-scale hubs step from their last guardian-signed setpoint (engine/ramp_anchor.py, see Utility calls); after a release such a hub is held at 0 kW until telemetry newer than the release arrives. See Safe stop.

Hub telemetry arrives every 10 s. A hub is stale after 25 s and offline after 60 s ([health]). The engine's cycle latency is exported on its loopback metrics port and alerted by ALR-CYCLE-P99 and ALR-CYCLE-P99-APPROACHING.

Work kept off the 2 s tick (r3.4.1 to r3.4.3): the PQ characterization pass (every 300 s) aggregates per hub in SQL and runs in a worker thread, bounded at 60 s; PQ waveform blob reads and writes and the trace journal's file sync also run off the event loop. This removed the periodic 4-5 s freezes of the engine loop seen in production.

Commitments and the lock (K13)

Once OpenGrid commits to a delivery, the delivery runs to completion. A better price arriving mid-contract never moves capacity away from a committed obligation (decision D-4).

Obligation states

og.obligation.state is one of OFFERED, SELECTED, COMMITTED, DELIVERING, FULFILLED, SHORTFALL, SETTLED, REJECTED, EXPIRED. AT_RISK is not a state but a flag (og.obligation.at_risk) set on top of the state, traced as AT_RISK / AT_RISK_CLEARED.

r3.4.3 fixes in the lock accounting: a hub in a firmware update is a device exclusion like a fault (substituted within the obligation; a shortfall it causes carries R-COMMIT-LOCK-OVERRIDE-L0), and G-19 drops such hubs from the bank capability it reads, so that claim corroborates. The K13 checker reads the current commitment of each obligation (not a superseded original) and judges capacity holds against the floor in force; see Capacity holds. A K4 re-solve keeps the original cycle id, so K13 sees one cycle.

Capacity holds: ancillary services and tolls

Some obligations sell availability, not energy. They are committed and reserved like any other obligation, but granted 0 kW until the buyer calls them. The reservation stays locked (K13) and enough energy is kept above reserve to answer the call.

ERCOT ancillary services (ERCOT_AS)

  1. Offer. Contract intake values an AS offer at the latest np4-188-cd clearing price (MCPC), only if that price is at most [contracts.intake].as_price_max_age_s (93600 s) old; otherwise nothing is offered that gate (INTAKE_SKIPPED, R-AS-PRICE-STALE).
  2. Award and hold. The selector commits the award. The allocator grants it 0 kW (R-GRANT-AS-HOLD), and the award keeps committed kW x product duration / discharge efficiency above the reserve floor (selector C3′ floor, engine hold floor). Durations come from the product rule: ECRS 60 min, Non-Spin (NSPIN) 240 min.
  3. Deployment. While an og.as_deployment row covers now, the allocator discharges the award up to its committed kW. Operators create one from the Dispatch page (Propose deployment, then Confirm deployment), which calls POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments. Since r3.4.1 that route, the utility API, the grid link and the AS poller all go through one core call function (opengrid.calls) with the same checks, and every call is recorded in og.dispatch_call with its origin (OPERATOR, UTILITY, GRID_LINK, ERCOT_POLL, ...). One award per deployment, capped at its product duration, no chaining; the engine caps the obligation at the call's requested kW. Stop deploy ends it early and the award returns to a 0 kW hold next cycle. The Dispatch page shows each deployment's origin.
  4. Settlement. Paid on the award. At r3.4.3 the capacity revenue of an ERCOT_AS award is priced at the cleared DAM MCPC for its product in the delivery hour (np4-188-cd, price flag MCPC); only when no MCPC observation exists does it fall back to the opportunity's stored value (flag OPPORTUNITY_PRICE, logged). The settlement trace records price_per_kwh and price_flag. If ALR-ENERGY-SHORTFALL-RISK was open during a settled interval, the settlement is flagged R-AS-HOLD-SHORT; the payment is unchanged.

K13 on holds (r3.4.3). The K13 checker takes the deployment windows of every capacity hold (ERCOT_AS, REGULATED_CAPACITY) from og.as_deployment. Outside a window the floor is 0 kW, so the 0 kW R-GRANT-AS-HOLD cycles of an undeployed hold are not violations (they were false K13_LOCK_VIOLATIONs before). Inside a window the floor is the requested kW, capped at the commitment, so a deployed hold that under-delivers is still a dip.

ERCOT's own deployment instructions reach OpenGrid only while og-feeds' AS poller is enabled ([feeds.ercot_as_poll], D-35; enabled = false in the repo). It reads DEPLOY_AS and RECALL_AS every 5 s over the ercot_mms adapter (the ogsim MMS simulator until go-live) and applies each through the same core as POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments (origin ERCOT_POLL; see ERCOT AS deployment instructions and AS-POLL). A refused instruction raises ALR-ERCOT-AS-REFUSED, failing polls ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-FAILED and no good poll for 60 s ALR-ERCOT-AS-POLL-STALE (playbook AS-POLL refusal). Otherwise deployments come only from an operator or a scenario. ERCOT market submission is built but off: nothing is submitted to ERCOT. The AS_HOLD invariant check measures only while a deployment is active.

Utility toll (D-29)

  • A toll is a REGULATED_CAPACITY contract with variant TOLLING (Austin Energy terms: $102/kW-yr, 90-minute product rule), paid on availability.
  • opengrid.contracts.tolling offers one obligation per day for the utility's window, [contracts.tolling] window_start 16:30 to window_end 18:00 America/Chicago, every day, for today (if not started) and tomorrow. The window length must equal the product rule's 90 minutes, or nothing is reserved.
  • The selector commits it as a regulated capacity hold; the allocator holds it at 0 kW until the utility calls.
  • A utility call goes through the same core call function as an AS deployment, capped at 90 minutes. The utility calls it itself through the utility API (D-33) or an operator issues it from the Dispatch page; the utility calls discharge only; charging follows local solar or the owner's schedule (D-30). See Utility calls.
  • The toll runs on the Austin substation battery (and the Austin home block where seeded). Its feeder has a 20 MW/min ramp ceiling so a 20 MW call reaches full output within one minute; the two Austin home feeders (feeder-LZ_AEN-00, -01) have 7 MW/min for the toll's home-bank share.
  • D-37: LCRA and RAYBURN have only sample toll contracts (Sample Contract: ..., is_sample, SUSPENDED, shown as SAMPLE – INACTIVE): no obligation is offered for them, they can never be called and are never billed.

Not implemented at r3.4.3: a true contract-term toll reservation (planned after R3).

Utility calls: utility API and grid link

Since r3.4.1 (D-33) a utility with a toll issues its own calls. Every call, from any origin, goes through one core function, opengrid.calls (issue_call, cancel_call, call_status), and is recorded in og.dispatch_call (migration 0047) next to the og.as_deployment row that the engine acts on. The full API reference is in the utility-API documents; see also Utility customer API.

  • States. The status carries delivered_kw, delivered_kwh, delivery_measured, delivery_state (the D-38 result) and meter_status. Before r3.4.3 it reported granted kW, not measured.
  • Engine side. The engine caps a called obligation at the call's requested kW. From r3.4.3 (engine/ramp_anchor.py) the substation set steps from its last signed setpoint (og.verdict PASS with published_at set, migration 0053, for the engine's own batch, while that lease is live), else from telemetry, by at most 0.9 × its ramp for one cycle. The guardian's G-04 uses the same anchor with a one-cycle bound, so a 20 MW call ramps at the firm hub rate within the 20 MW/min feeder ceiling (7 MW/min on the Austin home feeders). A lapsed lease or a safe stop drops the anchor and both sides re-anchor on telemetry; a veto does not (r3.4.3: the hub is still following its last signed setpoint, and re-anchoring on stale telemetry after a veto let an oversized step be signed); unsigned proposals never move it (the r3.4.2 behaviour stepped from its own unsigned proposals and was vetoed for the rest of the call after a signing gap). The engine reads the verdicts of its pending utility-scale batches once per cycle, bounded to 0.5 s.
  • Simulator. ogsim.utility_aen (unit og-sim-utility, Austin Energy) issues one call on hot days in the 16:30-18:00 window over the customer_api channel and logs the measured delivery; five /ogsim/ scenarios (utility_call_normal, _overlap, _over_cap, _charge, _cancel_mid) check the refusals. It skips a day with no toll reservation.

The grid link (D-34)

A DNP3 (IEEE 1815) outstation over mutual TLS (opengrid.integrations.grid_link, point list interfaces/grid_link/opengrid-gridlink-v1.json) through which a utility EMS sends toll calls (origin GRID_LINK, the same core) and L2 LIMIT/BLOCK per bank or zone, republished on scada/instruction as MQTT user og_gridlink. Peer address, certificate CN and point allow-lists apply; every inbound command is traced; heartbeat loss (heartbeat_timeout_s 30) fails safe; L2 levels are restored after a restart by replaying the link's trace. The Austin Energy simulator has a grid_link channel (a DNP3 master) as an alternative to the API.

The grid link is disabled by default: nothing listens unless [grid_link].enabled and the utility's own enabled are both true, and the shipped addresses and certificates are placeholders. deploy/scripts/grid_link_enable_loopback.sh enables a loopback test (test PKI, og_gridlink MQTT user, an og-engine drop-in setting OG_GRID_LINK_CONFIG). og-engine starts the link at startup (start_grid_link) once both switches are on; a link failure never stops the engine. See Grid link and the playbook Grid link heartbeat.

Measured delivery (D-38)

A grant is only what the allocator planned, and a guardian veto (for example G-04 on a ramp) means it never reaches a device. Every discharge call is therefore verified against what the batteries actually delivered, measured from telemetry. This covers utility toll calls, ERCOT AS deployments (each og.as_deployment) and operator manual discharge targets. og-settle's delivery job (opengrid.delivery, every [delivery].interval_s = 15 s) builds one og.delivery_record per call, in 30 s buckets aligned to the call start.

  • Committed is the call's requested kW, else the obligation's committed kW.
  • Commanded is the call's items in command batches the guardian signed. A vetoed batch commands 0 kW and is counted.
  • Delivered is the hubs' measured discharge. On a shared bank, only the obligation's share of the granted kW counts, the same rule settlement meters by.
  • Meter: where an independent meter exists (a substation asset's bank, such as bank-sub-LZ_AEN-00, or [delivery].meter_bank_ids), its REAL_POWER_KW change from a 120 s pre-call baseline is compared with the batteries' change.

The result is PASS, PARTIAL or FAIL (IN_PROGRESS while the call runs), with reasons:

  • RAMP_TOO_SLOW: not at target_frac (95 %) of committed within the product's ramp time ([delivery.ramp_time_s]: TOLLING 600 s, ECRS 600 s, RRS 600 s, REGUP/REGDN 300 s, NSPIN 1800 s, manual 120 s);
  • SUSTAIN_BELOW_TARGET: under 95 % of the buckets after the ramp at target;
  • NO_DELIVERY, ENERGY_SHORT (under half the committed kWh) and all-stale data give FAIL;
  • VETOED: at least half the command cycles were vetoed;
  • STOPPED: the call ended early;
  • DATA_STALE: over 10 % of the buckets had no telemetry.

The record also carries the time to target, the sustained compliance %, the lowest point and how long it lasted, and discharged against committed kWh.

Live alerts while a call runs: ALR-DELIVERY-RAMP-LATE (not at target after the ramp time), ALR-DELIVERY-SHORTFALL (below target for over shortfall_alert_s = 60 s) and ALR-DELIVERY-NONE (commanded, nothing measurable, for over none_alert_s = 60 s). While a SHORTFALL or NONE alert is open, the obligation is flagged AT_RISK (R-DELIVERY-MEASURED-SHORTFALL) and dispatch continues at the maximum feasible kW (K7, D-17). At the end of a call, a meter that disagrees by more than 10 % raises ALR-DELIVERY-METER-MISMATCH and marks the record UNCORROBORATED. That alert clears when the same meter agrees again on a later call, or when an operator clears it with a reason. After an og-settle restart, the job reconciles the AT_RISK flags it had set: a call still running short keeps its flag; any other flag is cleared, and so are the live alerts of calls that ended meanwhile. Products are matched by one canonical name (core.services.canonical_product), so NSPIN, NONSPIN and NON_SPIN all get Non-Spin's ramp time. The summary row is kept like the call (og.as_deployment); its per-bucket series is emptied after [delivery].series_keep_days (60 d).

Where it shows:

  • the Dispatch page's Delivery column and its detail drawer;
  • GET /og/api/delivery/records, /records/{call_id} and /summary;
  • a utility's own calls under /og/api/customer/v1/utility/delivery-records;
  • the utility call status (delivered_kw, RAMPING/DELIVERING);
  • the grid link's delivered-kW point.

Measured values trail real time by about 30-60 s (a 30 s telemetry lag plus the bucket).

Delivery verification (D-38)

From r3.4.3 delivery is verified from measurement, not from grants. og-settle's delivery job (opengrid.delivery, [delivery]) runs every 15 s and builds one og.delivery_record (migration 0050) per discharge call: a utility toll call or an ERCOT AS deployment (keyed by its deployment id) or a manual discharge target (keyed by its MANUAL_TARGET trace id). It is observation only (K7): it never changes dispatch.

  1. Series. The call window is cut into 30 s buckets (bucket_s), each evaluated 30 s late to wait for telemetry (telemetry_lag_s). Per bucket: committed kW, commanded kW (from the guardian-signed RT_ALLOCATION items of PASS verdicts) and delivered kW from hub telemetry, attributed to the call by grant share (the settlement rule). On an independently metered bank (every SUBSTATION asset bank, plus meter_bank_ids) the SCADA REAL_POWER_KW change is compared with the battery's.
  2. Metrics (opengrid.core.delivery, pure). Time to reach 95 % of committed (target_frac) against the product's ramp time ([delivery.ramp_time_s]: ECRS 600 s, RRS 600, REGUP/REGDN 300, NSPIN 1800, TOLLING 600, MANUAL 120), sustained compliance, the lowest point and how long it lasted, and delivered against committed kWh.
  3. Result. IN_PROGRESS while running, then PASS (no reason), FAIL (no delivery, energy short, or all data stale) or otherwise PARTIAL. Sustained compliance under 95 % (sustain_pass_pct) adds a reason. Reasons: RAMP_TOO_SLOW, SUSTAIN_BELOW_TARGET, NO_DELIVERY, VETOED, STOPPED, DATA_STALE, ENERGY_SHORT. Meter check: CORROBORATED, UNCORROBORATED (disagreement beyond 10 %, floor 25 kW), NO_METER or METER_STALE. The final record is traced once (stream delivery, event class DELIVERY_VERIFICATION). A call is followed until it has a final record, up to 1 h after it ended (lookback_s).

At the end of a call, a meter that disagrees by more than 10 % raises ALR-DELIVERY-METER-MISMATCH and marks the record UNCORROBORATED. That alert clears when the same meter agrees again on a later call, or when an operator clears it with a reason. After an og-settle restart, the job reconciles the AT_RISK flags it had set: a call still running short keeps its flag; any other flag is cleared, and so are the live alerts of calls that ended meanwhile. Products are matched by one canonical name (core.services.canonical_product), so NSPIN, NONSPIN and NON_SPIN all get Non-Spin's ramp time. The summary row is kept like the call (og.as_deployment); its per-bucket series is emptied after [delivery].series_keep_days (60 d).

Where it shows:

  • the Dispatch page's Delivery column and its detail drawer;
  • GET /og/api/delivery/records, /records/{call_id} and /summary;
  • a utility's own calls under /og/api/customer/v1/utility/delivery-records;
  • the utility call status (delivered_kw, RAMPING/DELIVERING);
  • the grid link's delivered-kW point.

Measured values trail real time by about 30-60 s (a 30 s telemetry lag plus the bucket).

Manual targets and commands

An operator can take hubs out of automatic dispatch and hold them at a chosen power. A manual target is not a one-shot command: it is a recorded intent that the engine ramps toward every cycle, with every step signed by the guardian.

Single hub or bank

  1. On the Fleet page choose a hub or bank, a setpoint in kW, a reason and optionally a duration, and press Propose (step 1 of 2). Nothing is commanded yet (POST /og/api/fleet/command); the proposal expires after 60 s.
  2. Confirm in the dialog (POST /og/api/fleet/command/{proposal_id}/confirm). The API writes a MANUAL_TARGET trace event (K10) and an og.operator_action row, and returns status RAMPING only once the MANUAL_TARGET row is durably in og.trace. Since r3.4.1 manual targets are written through a database-or-nothing trace store on their own stream (manual_target:<user>) and are never journaled, so a failed write cannot come back later through a journal replay. A lost acknowledgement is settled by looking the row up by request id: found gives 202, absent gives 503 "not recorded" (nothing ramps; retry when the database is back), and a failed lookup gives 503 "outcome unknown" (check the target list before retrying). If only the og.operator_action row fails after the target is durable, the answer is 202 with a warning.
  3. Every 2 s the engine steps each hub toward the target within the G-04 ramp limit (about p_kw/180 per cycle), reason R-MANUAL-RAMP, then holds it there. Home hubs step from their measured power; utility-scale hubs (the substation battery) step from the last setpoint the guardian signed (telemetry when there is none, after a veto or a stop), so a 20 MW call reaches full power at the G-04 rate.
  4. The target ends at expires_at: 15 minutes by default, at most 240. Cancel target ends it now (POST /og/api/fleet/manual-targets/{trace_id}/cancel); the hubs return to the allocator next cycle. The cancel acts only on hubs where that target is still ACTIVE: 404 for an unknown trace id, 409 (with the current statuses) when the target exists but has already ended, 503 when the cancel itself could not be recorded.

Target status: one rule

Since r3.4 one pure function, core.manual_targets.effective_targets, decides the status of every hub's newest target. The engine dispatches by it, GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets lists it, and the guardian uses it for its R-OPERATOR-OVERRIDE evidence, so the three can no longer disagree. Precedence: an operator or late-record cancel, then a safe stop, then expiry; otherwise ACTIVE.

CANCELLED_BY_SAFE_STOPA safe-stop ENGAGE covering the hub's bank, zone or the fleet was issued at or after the target, or a covering stop is still engaged; stop_event_id names the stop. A stop-cancelled target is never shown as active.

  • Sign convention everywhere: +charge / -discharge (+charge/-discharge). A target with any other convention is ignored (fail closed).
  • The newest target per hub wins. GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets lists each hub's newest target with its status, stop_event_id and cancelled_by.
  • A hub under an ACTIVE target is operator-owned: its committed kW moves to other hubs of the same obligation (K13 substitution; see the lock).
  • A safe stop covering the hub cancels the target for good (status CANCELLED_BY_SAFE_STOP); it does not resume after the release.
  • In a regulated or toll territory a manual discharge is vetoed by G-33 unless it serves that utility's own obligation; a manual charge or a 0 kW hold is allowed (D-29 c′). On an UNAVAILABLE bank (D-37) the API refuses a manual target with 409 and the owner's reason.
  • A confirmed manual discharge target is also verified from measurement like any other call (Delivery verification, ramp time 120 s).

Bulk commands

  1. Select hubs on the Fleet page and press Propose for selection (step 1 of 2) (POST /og/api/fleet/commands/bulk, at most 500 hubs by default). The response says whether a second confirmation is needed and why.
  2. Confirm (POST /og/api/fleet/commands/bulk/{proposal_id}/confirm). A second confirmation is required when any selected hub serves a COMMITTED/DELIVERING obligation (SERVES_COMMITTED_OBLIGATION), is faulted (HUB_FAULT), has a critical alert (CRITICAL_ALERT) or is at or below reserve (AT_OR_BELOW_RESERVE); the first confirm returns AWAITING_SECOND_CONFIRM. An offline hub (HUB_OFFLINE) is only a warning.
  3. The final confirm creates one manual target for the whole selection, exactly as for a single hub. Each step is traced as OPERATOR_BULK_COMMAND.

The second bulk confirmation may be given by the same operator; it is a deliberate re-acknowledgement, not a two-person control.

Safe stop

A safe stop ramps every hub in a scope to 0 kW and keeps it there until a release. It is run by og-safestop, a separate process with a stop-only Ed25519 key, so it works even when og-engine and og-guardian are down (K8). Scopes are FLEET, ZONE (a load zone) and BANK.

Engage (one operator, two steps)

  1. On the Fleet page choose the scope and a reason and press Propose safe stop (step 1 of 2) (POST /og/api/safestop). og-api sends a PROPOSE to og-safestop over the Postgres channel og_safestop_request. Nothing stops yet.
  2. Confirm within 30 s ([safestop].confirm_window_s; POST /og/api/safestop/{proposal_id}/confirm). og-safestop traces the stop, writes the og.stop_event ENGAGE row, and publishes a signed, retained message on <root>/stop/<scope>/<id>/<stop_id>. The API waits up to 3 s for the ENGAGE row and answers 503 if none appears (og-safestop down or its window elapsed); a proposal older than the window gets 409.

The stop outbox. At r3.4.3 the og.stop_event row and its og.stop_outbox entry are written in one transaction (ENGAGE and relayed RELEASE), so a stop can no longer be recorded without being queued. The outbox is re-published (QoS 1, retained) until the broker acknowledges it, including after a broker reconnect. Since r3.4.1 entries of one scope drain strictly in sequence order (so a RELEASE is never overtaken by an older ENGAGE of the same scope), and an ENGAGE goes first only across different scopes; since r3.4.3 a scope with a queued ENGAGE is read in full on every drain, so a stop never waits behind a backlog. A stop engaged while the broker is down is recorded and delivered as soon as the broker returns. og-safestop exports /metrics on loopback port 9106 ([metrics].safestop_port).

  • Redelivery repair. A redelivered utility L2 instruction that was already acted on but whose ENGAGE has no outbox row is re-signed under the same stop_id and queued (hubs de-duplicate by stop_id); never for a bank released since. A re-handed RELEASE that is already recorded is queued if missing.
  • Dead letter. Only permanent failures (a payload that can never be published) count as attempts; a broker outage never dead-letters a stop. At outbox_max_attempts (5) the entry is skipped, traced and the critical ALR-STOP-PUBLISH-DEAD-LETTER is raised once, so one poison entry never blocks later stops; it holds back only its scope's later RELEASEs, never an ENGAGE. Since r3.4.3 a dead-lettered ENGAGE is still retried after the live queue on every drain (traced DEAD_LETTER_PUBLISHED when it goes out). See the playbook Safe stop.

The simulated hubs ramp to 0 kW over 4 s (stop_ramp_s). The [safestop] keys ramp_bank_s, ramp_zone_s, ramp_fleet_s are reserved and not read by any code at r3.4.3.

Other ways a stop is engaged

  • Utility instruction. og-safestop engages a BANK stop for every unexpired L2 BLOCK or ESTOP instruction (initiator UTILITY). Operators cannot release such a stop.
  • Host CLI. When og-api is unavailable: python -m opengrid.safestop.cli engage with --confirm <SCOPE>:<ref>. It needs Postgres (K10) and has no release command.
  • Guardian request. After three escalations of a scope that keeps getting vetoes (K7), the guardian raises ALR-SAFE-STOP-REQUESTED and pre-fills the stop form. It never engages a stop itself.

Release (two different operators)

  1. Operator 1 presses Request release (operator 1) (POST /og/api/safestop/{scope}/{scope_id}/release). The request expires after 60 s.
  2. A different operator presses Review and approve (operator 2) (POST /og/api/safestop/release/{proposal_id}/approve). The requester is refused with 403. The API writes one confirmed SAFE_STOP_RELEASE operator action (tier TIER2).
  3. The guardian verifies it on its own: both operators on [guardian].stop_release_authorised_operators and distinct, approval at most 300 s old, request traced, scope still engaged, not a utility stop and no active L2 instruction. It then signs the RELEASE; og-safestop verifies the guardian signature and relays it on the stop's own topic. Since r3.4.3 og-safestop refuses a RELEASE signed before the newest ENGAGE on its scope (a newer stop arrived in between): nothing is relayed or recorded, the scope stays stopped, and ALR-STOP-RELEASE-SUPERSEDED asks the operators to re-issue the two-person release (see outbox).
  4. The API waits up to 10 s: 200 when the release landed, 202 while the guardian has not signed (it may refuse; this is never faked).

The engine during a stop (r3.4.3). og-engine honours og.stop_event: a bank under an engaged FLEET, ZONE or BANK stop gets no command batch, so no vetoed proposals pile up behind the stop. The stop also drops the signed ramp anchor of utility-scale hubs on both sides (engine and G-04). After the release a substation set is held at 0 kW until a telemetry sample newer than the release arrives, then it ramps again from telemetry at one cycle's G-04 step, so a toll call still running resumes without a burst of vetoes.

The stop-only key can never sign a release (opengrid.safestop.release always raises). See the playbook Safe stop.

Charging in each market

  • FREE: zone price plus M1 on grid-drawn kWh.
  • REGULATED: the utility's own charging terms (TOU off-peak or night rate), no M1, a 30 % soft solar floor, and grid charging only in the owner's windows. No charging while delivering a regulated obligation (no wash trade).
  • Capacity payment: settle pays a REGULATED contract its capacity price on availability, keyed on the contract's market (not on the asset's territory). Sample contracts are never billed. ERCOT_AS capacity is paid at the cleared MCPC.
  • Unavailable banks (D-37): no charging is planned there either; they are monitored only.
