How it works

Decision cycle

OpenGrid decides on two time scales. Every 15 minutes the selector decides what to sell and commit to over the next day. Every 2 seconds the allocator decides how much each bank delivers right now against what is already committed. The guardian checks and signs every resulting command before any battery moves.

At r3.4 og-feeds polls only the ERCOT products listed in [feeds.ercot].products (all six above in the shipped config; an unknown name stops og-feeds at startup, and disabled_products can still remove a listed one). A failed poll, or one skipped because the circuit breaker is open, is retried after 5 minutes, doubling per consecutive failure up to 60 minutes and never later than the product's normal cadence, so one missed daily np4-188-cd poll no longer leaves AS prices stale for a day.

og-feeds recomputes the forecast every 15 minutes: a 24-hour horizon at 15-minute resolution with quantiles 0.10, 0.50 and 0.90 (P10/P50/P90), for eight load-zone price and load series ([forecast]). A stale feed that is allowed to block firm commitments puts the system into NO_NEW_COMMITMENTS: existing commitments keep running, new ones are not made.

The 15-minute selector gate

  • og-engine checks the wall-clock 15-minute gate on each 2 s tick and runs selector.run_gate; it also runs gates for due ADMISSION and RENOMINATION triggers. Gate kinds in og.plan: SCHEDULED_15MIN, ADMISSION, RENOMINATION.
  • The model is a HiGHS MILP over banks x 96 intervals x P10/P50/P90 scenarios. Existing commitments are loaded frozen (K13, constraint C24). Regulated candidates are selected first (stage R), then the full net value on the rest (stage F).
  • Time limits: 45 s for the scheduled gate, 15 s for admission and re-nomination, target relative gap 1 %.
  • The solution is re-validated independently (K1, K2, K13, product rules). On infeasibility, a time-out without an acceptable gap, or a failed validation, the deterministic rule selector F2 is used instead (plan_mode RULE_FALLBACK). F2 also runs on every gate as the comparison baseline.
  • Each bank is priced at its own load zone. Wear is charged at [allocator].wear_usd_per_kwh = 0.03 on discharged kWh; charging cost includes M1 in the ERCOT competitive area (see Market model).
  • A failed gate is traced and raises ALR-SELECTOR-GATE-FAILED; the allocator keeps serving what is already committed.

The 2-second engine and allocator cycle

  1. Write the engine heartbeat (og.heartbeat).
  2. Flush the fleet twin (latest hub telemetry and health).
  3. Run any due selector gate (above).
  4. Run the allocator ([allocator].cycle_interval_s = 2; idle banks every 10 s). For every bank with an active obligation it re-derives how much of each committed kW is deliverable now, substitutes hubs within an obligation on health loss, applies the DIST_DEFERRAL PI loop, enforces flow limits and territory, then offers leftover headroom to the ERCOT energy market with a 300 s dwell (dwell_s) and a $5/MWh hysteresis (hysteresis_usd_per_mwh).
  5. If the guardian's heartbeat is current, write one og.command_batch per bank with grants and NOTIFY og_command_batch. If not, the cycle holds and no new batches are proposed.
  6. The guardian evaluates each batch on its own reads and returns PASS, PARTLY_VETOED, VETOED or TIMEOUT (og.verdict). Passed items are signed and published; a vetoed hub is excluded and re-solved next cycle (R-HUB-VETO-EXCLUDED).

Hub telemetry arrives every 10 s. A hub is stale after 25 s and offline after 60 s ([health]). The engine's cycle latency is exported on its loopback metrics port and alerted by ALR-CYCLE-P99 and ALR-CYCLE-P99-APPROACHING.

Commitments and the lock (K13)

Once OpenGrid commits to a delivery, the delivery runs to completion. A better price arriving mid-contract never moves capacity away from a committed obligation (decision D-4).

Obligation states

og.obligation.state is one of OFFERED, SELECTED, COMMITTED, DELIVERING, FULFILLED, SHORTFALL, SETTLED, REJECTED, EXPIRED. AT_RISK is not a state but a flag (og.obligation.at_risk) set on top of the state, traced as AT_RISK / AT_RISK_CLEARED.

Capacity holds: ancillary services and tolls

Some obligations sell availability, not energy. They are committed and reserved like any other obligation, but granted 0 kW until the buyer calls them. The reservation stays locked (K13) and enough energy is kept above reserve to answer the call.

ERCOT ancillary services (ERCOT_AS)

  1. Offer. Contract intake values an AS offer at the latest np4-188-cd clearing price (MCPC), only if that price is at most [contracts.intake].as_price_max_age_s (93600 s) old; otherwise nothing is offered that gate (INTAKE_SKIPPED, R-AS-PRICE-STALE).
  2. Award and hold. The selector commits the award. The allocator grants it 0 kW (R-GRANT-AS-HOLD), and the award keeps committed kW x product duration / discharge efficiency above the reserve floor (selector C3′ floor, engine hold floor). Durations come from the product rule: ECRS 60 min, Non-Spin (NSPIN) 240 min.
  3. Deployment. While an og.as_deployment row covers now, the allocator discharges the award up to its committed kW. Operators create one from the Dispatch page (Propose deployment, then Confirm deployment), which calls POST /og/api/dispatch/as-deployments. One award per deployment, capped at its product duration, no chaining; Stop deploy ends it early and the award returns to a 0 kW hold next cycle.
  4. Settlement. Paid on the award. If ALR-ENERGY-SHORTFALL-RISK was open during a settled interval, the settlement is flagged R-AS-HOLD-SHORT; the payment is unchanged.

At r3.4 there is no live ERCOT deployment signal. Deployments come only from an operator, a scenario, or the market simulator's poller, which is off ([market_sim].as_deployment_poll = false). ERCOT market submission (ercot_mms) is built but off. The AS_HOLD invariant check measures only while a deployment is active.

Utility toll (D-29)

  • A toll is a REGULATED_CAPACITY contract with variant TOLLING (Austin Energy terms: $102/kW-yr, 90-minute product rule), paid on availability.
  • opengrid.contracts.tolling offers one obligation per day for the utility's window, [contracts.tolling] window_start 16:30 to window_end 18:00 America/Chicago, every day, for today (if not started) and tomorrow. The window length must equal the product rule's 90 minutes, or nothing is reserved.
  • The selector commits it as a regulated capacity hold; the allocator holds it at 0 kW until the utility calls.
  • A utility call uses the same deployment route as AS (reason prefixed "utility call"), capped at 90 minutes. The utility calls discharge only; charging follows local solar or the owner's schedule (D-30).
  • The toll runs on the Austin substation battery (and the Austin home block where seeded). Its feeder has a 20 MW/min ramp ceiling so a 20 MW call reaches full output within one minute.

Not implemented at r3.4: a true contract-term toll reservation (planned after R3).

Measured delivery (D-38)

A grant is only what the allocator planned, and a guardian veto (for example G-04 on a ramp) means it never reaches a device. Every discharge call is therefore verified against what the batteries actually delivered, measured from telemetry. This covers utility toll calls, ERCOT AS deployments (each og.as_deployment) and operator manual discharge targets. og-settle's delivery job (opengrid.delivery, every [delivery].interval_s = 15 s) builds one og.delivery_record per call, in 30 s buckets aligned to the call start.

  • Committed is the call's requested kW, else the obligation's committed kW.
  • Commanded is the call's items in command batches the guardian signed. A vetoed batch commands 0 kW and is counted.
  • Delivered is the hubs' measured discharge. On a shared bank, only the obligation's share of the granted kW counts, the same rule settlement meters by.
  • Meter: where an independent meter exists (a substation asset's bank, such as bank-sub-LZ_AEN-00, or [delivery].meter_bank_ids), its REAL_POWER_KW change from a 120 s pre-call baseline is compared with the batteries' change.

The result is PASS, PARTIAL or FAIL (IN_PROGRESS while the call runs), with reasons:

  • RAMP_TOO_SLOW: not at target_frac (95 %) of committed within the product's ramp time ([delivery.ramp_time_s]: TOLLING 600 s, ECRS 600 s, RRS 600 s, REGUP/REGDN 300 s, NSPIN 1800 s, manual 120 s);
  • SUSTAIN_BELOW_TARGET: under 95 % of the buckets after the ramp at target;
  • NO_DELIVERY, ENERGY_SHORT (under half the committed kWh) and all-stale data give FAIL;
  • VETOED: at least half the command cycles were vetoed;
  • STOPPED: the call ended early;
  • DATA_STALE: over 10 % of the buckets had no telemetry.

The record also carries the time to target, the sustained compliance %, the lowest point and how long it lasted, and discharged against committed kWh.

Live alerts while a call runs: ALR-DELIVERY-RAMP-LATE (not at target after the ramp time), ALR-DELIVERY-SHORTFALL (below target for over shortfall_alert_s = 60 s) and ALR-DELIVERY-NONE (commanded, nothing measurable, for over none_alert_s = 60 s). While a SHORTFALL or NONE alert is open, the obligation is flagged AT_RISK (R-DELIVERY-MEASURED-SHORTFALL) and dispatch continues at the maximum feasible kW (K7, D-17). At the end of a call, a meter that disagrees by more than 10 % raises ALR-DELIVERY-METER-MISMATCH and marks the record UNCORROBORATED.

Where it shows:

  • the Dispatch page's Delivery column and its detail drawer;
  • GET /og/api/delivery/records, /records/{call_id} and /summary;
  • a utility's own calls under /og/api/customer/v1/utility/delivery-records;
  • the utility call status (delivered_kw, RAMPING/DELIVERING);
  • the grid link's delivered-kW point.

Measured values trail real time by about 30-60 s (a 30 s telemetry lag plus the bucket).

Manual targets and commands

An operator can take hubs out of automatic dispatch and hold them at a chosen power. A manual target is not a one-shot command: it is a recorded intent that the engine ramps toward every cycle, with every step signed by the guardian.

Single hub or bank

  1. On the Fleet page choose a hub or bank, a setpoint in kW, a reason and optionally a duration, and press Propose (step 1 of 2). Nothing is commanded yet (POST /og/api/fleet/command); the proposal expires after 60 s.
  2. Confirm in the dialog (POST /og/api/fleet/command/{proposal_id}/confirm). The API writes a MANUAL_TARGET trace event (K10) and an og.operator_action row, and returns status RAMPING only once the MANUAL_TARGET row is durably in og.trace. If the trace backend fell back to its local journal (database unreachable), the confirm answers 503 "not recorded", writes no operator action and nothing ramps; retry when the database is back.
  3. Every 2 s the engine steps each hub toward the target within the G-04 ramp limit (about p_kw/180 per cycle), reason R-MANUAL-RAMP, then holds it there. Home hubs step from their measured power; utility-scale hubs (the substation battery) step from the last commanded setpoint, so a 20 MW call reaches full power at the G-04 rate.
  4. The target ends at expires_at: 15 minutes by default, at most 240. Cancel target ends it now (POST /og/api/fleet/manual-targets/{trace_id}/cancel); the hubs return to the allocator next cycle. The cancel acts only on hubs where that target is still ACTIVE: 404 for an unknown trace id, 409 (with the current statuses) when the target exists but has already ended, 503 when the cancel itself could not be recorded.

Target status: one rule

At r3.4 one pure function, core.manual_targets.effective_targets, decides the status of every hub's newest target. The engine dispatches by it, GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets lists it, and the guardian uses it for its R-OPERATOR-OVERRIDE evidence, so the three can no longer disagree. Precedence: an operator or late-record cancel, then a safe stop, then expiry; otherwise ACTIVE.

  • Sign convention everywhere: +charge / -discharge (+charge/-discharge). A target with any other convention is ignored (fail closed).
  • The newest target per hub wins. GET /og/api/fleet/manual-targets lists each hub's newest target with its status, stop_event_id and cancelled_by.
  • A hub under an ACTIVE target is operator-owned: its committed kW moves to other hubs of the same obligation (K13 substitution; see the lock).
  • A safe stop covering the hub cancels the target for good (status CANCELLED_BY_SAFE_STOP); it does not resume after the release.
  • In a regulated or toll territory a manual discharge is vetoed by G-33 unless it serves that utility's own obligation; a manual charge or a 0 kW hold is allowed (D-29 c′).

Bulk commands

  1. Select hubs on the Fleet page and press Propose for selection (step 1 of 2) (POST /og/api/fleet/commands/bulk, at most 500 hubs by default). The response says whether a second confirmation is needed and why.
  2. Confirm (POST /og/api/fleet/commands/bulk/{proposal_id}/confirm). A second confirmation is required when any selected hub serves a COMMITTED/DELIVERING obligation (SERVES_COMMITTED_OBLIGATION), is faulted (HUB_FAULT), has a critical alert (CRITICAL_ALERT) or is at or below reserve (AT_OR_BELOW_RESERVE); the first confirm returns AWAITING_SECOND_CONFIRM. An offline hub (HUB_OFFLINE) is only a warning.
  3. The final confirm creates one manual target for the whole selection, exactly as for a single hub. Each step is traced as OPERATOR_BULK_COMMAND.

The second bulk confirmation may be given by the same operator; it is a deliberate re-acknowledgement, not a two-person control.

Safe stop

A safe stop ramps every hub in a scope to 0 kW and keeps it there until a release. It is run by og-safestop, a separate process with a stop-only Ed25519 key, so it works even when og-engine and og-guardian are down (K8). Scopes are FLEET, ZONE (a load zone) and BANK.

Engage (one operator, two steps)

  1. On the Fleet page choose the scope and a reason and press Propose safe stop (step 1 of 2) (POST /og/api/safestop). og-api sends a PROPOSE to og-safestop over the Postgres channel og_safestop_request. Nothing stops yet.
  2. Confirm within 30 s ([safestop].confirm_window_s; POST /og/api/safestop/{proposal_id}/confirm). og-safestop traces the stop, writes the og.stop_event ENGAGE row, and publishes a signed, retained message on <root>/stop/<scope>/<id>/<stop_id>. The API waits up to 3 s for the ENGAGE row and answers 503 if none appears (og-safestop down or its window elapsed); a proposal older than the window gets 409.

The stop outbox. At r3.4 the og.stop_event row and its og.stop_outbox entry are written in one transaction (ENGAGE and relayed RELEASE), so a stop can no longer be recorded without being queued. The outbox is re-published (QoS 1, retained) until the broker acknowledges it, including after a broker reconnect; every queued ENGAGE is drained before older RELEASEs, each oldest first. A stop engaged while the broker is down is recorded and delivered as soon as the broker returns.

  • Redelivery repair. A redelivered utility L2 instruction that was already acted on but whose ENGAGE has no outbox row is re-signed under the same stop_id and queued (hubs de-duplicate by stop_id); never for a bank released since. A re-handed RELEASE that is already recorded is queued if missing.
  • Dead letter. Only permanent failures (a payload that can never be published) count as attempts; a broker outage never dead-letters a stop. At outbox_max_attempts (5) the entry is skipped, traced and the critical ALR-STOP-PUBLISH-DEAD-LETTER is raised once, so one poison entry never blocks later stops. See the playbook Safe stop.

The simulated hubs ramp to 0 kW over 4 s (stop_ramp_s). The [safestop] keys ramp_bank_s, ramp_zone_s, ramp_fleet_s are reserved and not read by any code at r3.4.

Other ways a stop is engaged

  • Utility instruction. og-safestop engages a BANK stop for every unexpired L2 BLOCK or ESTOP instruction (initiator UTILITY). Operators cannot release such a stop.
  • Host CLI. When og-api is unavailable: python -m opengrid.safestop.cli engage with --confirm <SCOPE>:<ref>. It needs Postgres (K10) and has no release command.
  • Guardian request. After three escalations of a scope that keeps getting vetoes (K7), the guardian raises ALR-SAFE-STOP-REQUESTED and pre-fills the stop form. It never engages a stop itself.

Release (two different operators)

  1. Operator 1 presses Request release (operator 1) (POST /og/api/safestop/{scope}/{scope_id}/release). The request expires after 60 s.
  2. A different operator presses Review and approve (operator 2) (POST /og/api/safestop/release/{proposal_id}/approve). The requester is refused with 403. The API writes one confirmed SAFE_STOP_RELEASE operator action (tier TIER2).
  3. The guardian verifies it on its own: both operators on [guardian].stop_release_authorised_operators and distinct, approval at most 300 s old, request traced, scope still engaged, not a utility stop and no active L2 instruction. It then signs the RELEASE; og-safestop verifies the guardian signature and relays it on the stop's own topic.
  4. The API waits up to 10 s: 200 when the release landed, 202 while the guardian has not signed (it may refuse; this is never faked).

The stop-only key can never sign a release (opengrid.safestop.release always raises). See the playbook Safe stop.

Charging in each market

  • FREE: zone price plus M1 on grid-drawn kWh.
  • REGULATED: the utility's own charging terms (TOU off-peak or night rate), no M1, a 30 % soft solar floor, and grid charging only in the owner's windows. No charging while delivering a regulated obligation (no wash trade).
  • Capacity payment: settle pays a REGULATED contract its capacity price on availability, keyed on the contract's market (not on the asset's territory).
