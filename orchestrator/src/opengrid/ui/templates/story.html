What this system is for

The orchestrator sells each kilowatt-hour once, to the buyer the contracts say should have it, proves it was delivered, and measures what that is worth against the rule-based allocation it replaces.

One home battery is a backup-power product. Thousands of them are a power plant that several kinds of buyer want at the same hour. The hard part is not selling the energy. It is arbitrating competing claims on the same kilowatt safely, keeping every home's outage reserve out of the trade, and being able to explain every decision afterwards.

The money it turned down

Why this is a decision engine, not a dashboard

A dashboard shows you the price. This system decides, every two seconds, whether to chase it, and keeps a record of the value it declined. When the wholesale price spikes during a delivery, committed customers keep their capacity and the spike goes uncaptured. That forgone money is measured, not hidden, because keeping a promise has a cost and the cost belongs on the books.
